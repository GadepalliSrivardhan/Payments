5.1 SWIFT Network Overview

SWIFT (Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication) is a member-owned cooperative founded in 1973 and headquartered in La Hulpe, Belgium. It operates the world's leading secure financial messaging network, connecting over 11,000 financial institutions in more than 200 countries and territories. SWIFT does not move money — it carries messages (instructions) between financial institutions. The actual movement of funds happens through correspondent banking relationships and local settlement systems (RTGS, ACH, etc.).

Key Principle: SWIFT is a messaging network only. When Bank A sends a SWIFT MT 103 to Bank B, it is instructing Bank B to pay the beneficiary. The actual debit/credit happens via the nostro/vostro relationship and settlement through a clearing system — not through SWIFT itself.

SWIFT for Corporates

Traditionally SWIFT was a bank-only network. Today, large corporates can connect directly to SWIFT via several service models, bypassing proprietary bank channels and enabling multi-bank treasury management from a single connection:

🔒

MA-CUG

Member-Administered Closed User Group. A bank sets up a restricted group on SWIFTNet and invites its corporate clients to join. The bank administers membership. The corporate can only communicate with the banks in that group — it is the most restricted (and cheapest) model, suitable for corporates that bank with a small number of institutions.

🌐

SCORE

Standardised CORporate Environment. An open, industry-wide connectivity model where any SWIFT-connected corporate can communicate with any SWIFT-connected bank, using standardised message formats. SCORE enables true multi-bank connectivity without separate bilateral agreements for each bank relationship.

📁

FileAct

Bulk file transfer service. FileAct allows large files (e.g., bulk payment batches, account statements, salary runs) to be transferred securely over SWIFTNet. Supports structured (ISO 20022 XML) and unstructured file formats. Used for high-volume, lower-urgency payment initiation (pain.001 batches) and large MT 101 files from corporates.

⚡

InterAct

Real-time interactive messaging. InterAct supports store-and-forward and real-time request/response messaging patterns over SWIFTNet. Used for individual payment instructions (MT 103, pacs.008), account queries, and status requests. InterAct is the channel for time-critical, transactional messages.

📡

SWIFTNet

The secure IP-based network over which all SWIFT messages travel. Uses PKI encryption and digital signatures to ensure message integrity and non-repudiation. All traffic is encrypted end-to-end.

🏦

Alliance Platforms

Alliance Lite2, Alliance Access, Alliance Messaging Hub (AMH) — SWIFT's software products for connecting institutions to SWIFTNet. Alliance Lite2 is the cloud-based entry-level offering; AMH is the enterprise-grade message management platform.

🛡️

Customer Security Programme (CSP)

Mandatory security framework for all SWIFT-connected institutions, launched after the 2016 Bangladesh Bank heist. Defines mandatory and advisory security controls — including MFA, network segregation, and anomaly detection — enforced via the Customer Security Controls Framework (CSCF).

5.2 SWIFT MT Message Categories

SWIFT MT (Message Type) messages are structured financial messages using SWIFT's proprietary text-based format. Each message has a 3-digit type number. Messages are grouped into categories (1xx, 2xx, 9xx, etc.) by business function. The most important categories for payments professionals are 1xx (customer payments), 2xx (financial institution transfers), and 9xx (cash management and statements).

Category 1xx — Customer Payments and Cheques

Category 1 messages carry customer (non-bank) payment instructions — the originator or beneficiary is a corporate or individual, not a financial institution.

MT TypeNameKey Use
MT 101Request for TransferCorporate-to-bank: a corporate instructs its bank to execute multiple credit transfers on its behalf. Supports multiple payment instructions in a single message (bulk initiation). The ISO 20022 equivalent is pain.001.
MT 103Single Customer Credit TransferBank-to-bank: transfer funds on behalf of a customer. The most widely used cross-border payment message on SWIFT. Each MT 103 carries a single payment instruction. The ISO 20022 equivalent is pacs.008.
MT 103 STPSingle Customer Credit Transfer (STP)Straight-Through Processing variant of the MT 103. All mandatory fields are fully populated for automated processing with no manual intervention. Tag :23B: must be CRED; specific field constraints apply to prevent breaks in the STP chain.
MT 110Advice of ChequeSent by a bank to advise its correspondent of a cheque it has issued. Provides details of the cheque so the correspondent can expect presentment.
MT 111Request for Stop Payment of a ChequeInstructions to stop payment on a specific cheque. Sent urgently when fraud or loss is suspected.
MT 112Status of a Request for Stop PaymentResponse to an MT 111, confirming whether the stop payment was actioned and whether the cheque had already been paid.
MT 104Direct Debit / Request for Debit TransferMultiple direct debit instructions in a single message. Used for recurring collections (e.g., insurance premiums, utilities). Less common in cross-border context.

Category 2xx — Financial Institution Transfers

Category 2 messages carry interbank fund transfers — the payer and payee are both financial institutions. These messages move liquidity between banks and provide cover for customer payment chains.

MT TypeNameKey Use
MT 200Financial Institution Transfer for Own AccountA bank transfers its own funds between two of its accounts held at different correspondent banks. No third-party beneficiary — purely a treasury/liquidity management movement.
MT 201Multiple Financial Institution Transfers for Own AccountMultiple MT 200-type instructions consolidated into a single message for efficiency.
MT 202General Financial Institution TransferBank-to-bank transfer where the beneficiary is a financial institution. Used for interbank settlements, nostro funding, and as the cover payment mechanism (pre-FATF). Does not contain underlying customer details.
MT 202 COVCover PaymentThe transparency-enhanced variant of MT 202, introduced in 2009 following FATF Recommendation 16 requirements. Used when an MT 103 is sent directly to the beneficiary bank while funds travel through correspondents. The MT 202 COV carries underlying customer details (originator and beneficiary) in its mandatory Sequence B — ensuring the entire chain has access to the underlying transaction information for AML/sanctions screening. Banks must not use a plain MT 202 where an MT 202 COV is required.
MT 203Multiple General Financial Institution TransfersMultiple MT 202 instructions in a single message to different beneficiary institutions.
MT 205Financial Institution Transfer ExecutionUsed when a third bank in the correspondent chain executes the transfer on behalf of the ordering bank. Carries the original MT 202 instructions forward in the chain.
MT 202 COV — Mandatory Sequence B: When banks use a serial payment structure (MT 103 sent directly to beneficiary bank via one or more correspondents that also receive separate MT 202 cover payments), each cover payment MUST use MT 202 COV and populate Sequence B with: :50a (Ordering Customer), :52a (Ordering Institution), :56a (Intermediary Institution, if applicable), :57a (Account With Institution), and :59a (Beneficiary Customer). This ensures every correspondent in the cover chain can screen the underlying customer details against sanctions lists.

Category 9xx — Cash Management and Customer Statements

Category 9 messages support account reporting, reconciliation, and cash management. Banks send these to their corporate and correspondent banking clients to report on account activity.

MT TypeNameKey Use
MT 900Confirmation of DebitSent by a bank to a correspondent to confirm a debit made to the correspondent's account. Used in nostro reconciliation — the respondent bank uses MT 900s to reconcile its nostro mirror ledger.
MT 910Confirmation of CreditConfirms a credit to a correspondent's account. The credit-side equivalent of MT 900. Essential for same-day nostro reconciliation and intraday liquidity management.
MT 920Request MessageSent to a bank to request an MT 940, MT 941, or MT 942 statement. Allows on-demand account reporting rather than waiting for end-of-day delivery.
MT 940Customer Statement MessageEnd-of-day account statement for a nostro/vostro account, sent by the account-holding bank to the account owner. Contains all transactions during the day plus opening and closing balances. ISO 20022 equivalent: camt.053.
MT 942Interim Transaction ReportIntraday account statement — sent during the business day to report transactions posted since the last statement (opening or previous MT 942). Critical for intraday liquidity management and nostro reconciliation. ISO 20022 equivalent: camt.052.
MT 950Statement MessageSimilar to MT 940 but used for non-nostro accounts. Provides opening balance, transactions, and closing balance. May also be used for end-of-day customer account statements.

5.3 MT 103 Field-by-Field Breakdown

The MT 103 Single Customer Credit Transfer is the workhorse of cross-border payments — it is the message sent bank-to-bank when transferring funds on behalf of a customer. Understanding every field is essential for payment operations, compliance, and the MT-to-MX migration.

Format Notation: SWIFT uses a shorthand for field lengths. 16x = up to 16 characters (alphanumeric). 4!a = exactly 4 alphabetic characters. 3n = up to 3 numeric digits. Exclamation mark (!) means exact — the field must be exactly that length.

:23E:Instruction CodeOptional4!a[/30x]Additional handling instructions. Common codes: CHQB (pay by cheque), HOLD (hold at beneficiary bank for collection), INTC (intra-company payment), REPA (related to a repo), SDVA (same-day value). Repeatable up to 3 times.

Maps to MX /PrvsInstgAgt (Previous Instructing Agent). Critical for understanding the reimbursement chain.
:54A / 54B / 54DReceiver's CorrespondentOptionalVariousThe institution through which the receiver of the MT 103 will be reimbursed — the account-holding bank for the receiver's nostro. :54A: BIC; :54B: location; :54D: name/address. Maps to MX /IntrmyAgt (Intermediary Agent). Used when the receiver does not maintain a nostro with the sender directly.
:55A / 55B / 55DThird Reimbursement InstitutionOptionalVariousA third institution involved in reimbursing the receiver's correspondent (in a four-bank chain). Rarely used in practice but present in complex multi-correspondent chains. Maps to MX /IntrmyAgt2 or equivalent third-party reimbursement agent element.
:56A / 56C / 56DIntermediary InstitutionOptionalVariousAn institution in the payment chain between the receiver of the MT 103 and the account-with institution (beneficiary's bank). The MT 103 receiver passes the payment to this institution, which then forwards to field :57: / beneficiary bank. :56A: BIC; :56C: account number; :56D: name/address. Maps to MX /IntrmyAgt.
:57A / 57B / 57C / 57DAccount With InstitutionOptionalVariousThe financial institution at which the beneficiary customer maintains their account — the beneficiary's bank. :57A: BIC (most common); :57B: location; :57C: account number of the beneficiary's bank at an intermediary; :57D: name/address. Maps to MX /CdtrAgt (Creditor Agent). Absence of this field implies the receiver of the MT 103 is itself the beneficiary's bank.
:59 / 59A / 59FBeneficiary CustomerMandatoryVariousThe ultimate recipient of the funds. :59: — account number + name/address (up to 4×35 chars, free text); :59A: — BIC of the beneficiary (institutional beneficiary); :59F: — structured format using sub-fields for party identifier (account/IBAN), name, and structured address components (town, country). :59F: is strongly preferred for STP and ISO 20022 migration readiness. Maps to MX /Cdtr + /CdtrAcct.
:70:Remittance InformationOptional4×35xFree-text remittance information to help the beneficiary identify the purpose of the payment — invoice references, purchase order numbers, description. Maximum 4 lines of 35 characters each (140 characters total). SWIFT code words in this field: /INV/ (invoice), /IPI/ (international payment instruction reference), /ROC/ (ordering customer's reference), /ULTD/ (ultimate debtor), /ULTC/ (ultimate creditor). Maps to MX /RmtInf.
:71A:Details of ChargesMandatory3!aDefines who bears the transaction charges throughout the payment chain. Three options: OUR — all charges (including correspondent and beneficiary bank charges) are borne by the ordering customer; the beneficiary receives the full instructed amount. SHA (shared) — sender's bank charges paid by the ordering customer; all other charges paid by the beneficiary. SHA is the most common value in cross-border payments. BEN — all charges borne by the beneficiary; the beneficiary receives the amount less all charges. Maps to MX /ChrgBr with values DEBT (OUR), SHAR (SHA), CRED (BEN).
:71F:Sender's ChargesOptional3!a15dThe currency and amount of charges deducted by the sender. Populated when :71A: is SHA or BEN so that downstream banks understand what has already been deducted. Repeatable for multiple charge items.
:71G:Receiver's ChargesOptional3!a15dThe currency and amount of charges to be collected by the receiver. Present only when :71A: is OUR, indicating the receiver will collect these charges and must be reimbursed for them.
:72:Sender to Receiver InformationOptional6×35xFree-text field for bank-to-bank instructions not covered by other fields. Up to 6 lines of 35 characters (210 characters). Common structured code words: /ACC/ (account with instruction), /BNF/ (beneficiary), /INS/ (instructing institution — used to carry the instructing party BIC when no dedicated field exists in MT), /INT/ (intermediary), /REC/ (regulatory). In the MT-to-MX migration, /INS/ in :72: maps to MX /PrvsInstgAgt. Maps partly to MX /InstrForCdtrAgt and /InstrForNxtAgt.
:77B:Regulatory ReportingOptional3×35xUsed to carry regulatory reporting codes required by the laws of specific countries (e.g., purpose codes for India's RBI reporting, CIPS codes for China, balance-of-payments codes). Format: /BENEFRES/ or /ORDERRES/ followed by the 2-letter country code and the regulatory code. Up to 3 lines of 35 characters. Maps to MX /RgltryRptg.

5.4 ISO 20022 Message Format

ISO 20022 is an international standard for financial messaging developed by ISO (International Organisation for Standardisation). Unlike SWIFT MT messages which use a proprietary text-based format, ISO 20022 uses XML (eXtensible Markup Language) — a structured, data-rich, machine-readable format. ISO 20022 messages carry structured data (legal names, structured addresses, LEIs, purpose codes) rather than free text, enabling far better straight-through processing, fraud detection, and regulatory compliance.

ISO 20022 Identifier Format

Every ISO 20022 message has a unique identifier following a strict 4-part format:

Format: xxxx . nnn . aaa . bb Example: pacs . 008 . 001 . 08 ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑ Family Msg-Type Variant Version
ComponentLengthNameDescriptionExample
xxxx4 alphabetic (4!a)Message Family / Business ProcessA 4-letter code identifying the broad business area or process the message belongs to. Each family covers a specific domain of financial activity.pacs, pain, camt, fxtr
nnn3 alphanumeric (3!c)Message Functionality (Type)A 3-character code (usually numeric) identifying the specific message type within the family. Each number represents a distinct message with a defined business purpose.008 = FI-to-FI Customer Credit Transfer
aaa3 numeric (3!n)VariantIdentifies a specific market practice variant of the message. The base standard always uses 001. Some jurisdictions define additional variants (e.g., 002, 003) for local requirements.001 (always 001 for standard CBPR+ messages)
bb2 numeric (2!n)VersionVersion number, incremented each time the message schema is updated. Higher version = more recent. CBPR+ mandates a minimum version for cross-border use.08 = Version 8 of pacs.008.001
Reading pacs.008.001.08: pacs = Payment Clearing & Settlement | 008 = FI to FI Customer Credit Transfer | 001 = Standard Variant | 08 = Version 8. This is the ISO 20022 equivalent of the MT 103 and is the primary message used in CBPR+ cross-border payments today.

Key ISO 20022 Message Families

ISO 20022 messages are organised into message families (business domains). The four families most relevant to payments are:

💳

pain — PAyment INitiation

Customer-to-bank (C2B) payment initiation messages. A corporate or retail customer uses pain messages to instruct their bank to make payments. The bank then uses pacs messages for the interbank leg.

⚙️

pacs — PAyment Clearing & Settlement

Interbank payment clearing and settlement messages. Used exclusively between financial institutions. The core messages for cross-border payments (pacs.008) and returns (pacs.004) live here.

🏦

camt — CAsh ManagemenT

Account reporting, statement, and investigation messages. Used both C2B (account statements to corporates) and interbank (investigations, recalls, responses). Covers the camt.05x statement family and the camt.02x/056/087 investigation family.

💱

fxtr — FX TRade

Foreign exchange trade confirmation and settlement messages. Used to confirm FX deals and manage the settlement of FX transactions between financial institutions.

ISO 20022 Cardinality — Mandatory vs Optional

In XML/ISO 20022 messages, how many times an element must or may appear is defined by Min and Max values in the XSD (XML Schema Definition):

MinMaxMeaningExample
01Optional — may appear at most onceIntermediaryAgent1 in pacs.008 (optional intermediary)
0*Optional — may appear zero or more timesChargesInformation (zero or more charge items)
11Mandatory — must appear exactly onceGroupHeader in pacs.008
1*Mandatory — one or more repetitionsCreditTransferTransactionInformation (at least one payment)
Important — Context Dependency: Cardinality must be read in context. If a parent element is optional (Min=0), all its children are also effectively optional in the full message — even if they show Min=1 at their own level. Always trace the full XPath hierarchy. Also note Choice elements (XOR constraints): exactly one child of a choice group must be present. For example, the Creditor identification is a choice between IBAN or Other account identification — only one may be populated.

5.6 CBPR+ and SWIFT GPI

CBPR+ — Cross-Border Payments and Reporting Plus

CBPR+ is SWIFT's usage guideline for ISO 20022 in cross-border payments. ISO 20022 is a global standard, but it is highly flexible — different communities can use the same messages in different ways. CBPR+ defines the specific rules, field constraints, business validations, and address requirements that SWIFT member banks must follow when exchanging cross-border ISO 20022 messages on SWIFTNet. Think of CBPR+ as the "rulebook on top of the standard."

📋

Usage Guidelines

CBPR+ publishes detailed usage guidelines for each supported message (pacs.008, pacs.009, pacs.004, camt.056, etc.) that define: which fields are mandatory/optional within the CBPR+ community; which code values are permitted; character set restrictions; and cross-field business rules that go beyond the XSD schema.

🔗

MyStandards

SWIFT's MyStandards portal is the authoritative source for CBPR+ rules. Banks use MyStandards to check field-by-field usage rules, validate messages against CBPR+ business rules, and generate test cases. It is the primary reference for payment engineers building ISO 20022 processing engines.

📅

Standards Release (SR)

SWIFT publishes an annual Standards Release (SR) each November. SR introduces new message versions, updated business rules, and new features. SR2025 (November 2025) marked the end of the MT/MX coexistence period and brought the hybrid address changes.

Hybrid Address — SR2025

A key CBPR+ topic is the handling of postal addresses in MX messages. ISO 20022 supports structured addresses (individual XML elements for street, town, country) and unstructured addresses (free-text AdrLine elements). CBPR+ rules evolved significantly across Standards Releases:

SWIFT GPI — Global Payments Innovation

SWIFT GPI, launched in 2017, transformed cross-border payments by introducing end-to-end tracking, same-day settlement, fee transparency, and universal confirmation. GPI is now mandatory for all SWIFT member banks making cross-border payments.

UETR — Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference

The cornerstone of GPI is the UETR (Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference). The UETR is a UUID (Universally Unique Identifier) in the format xxxxxxxx-xxxx-xxxx-xxxx-xxxxxxxxxxxx (36 characters, 32 hex digits + 4 hyphens). The UETR is:

  • Generated by the first bank in the payment chain (the debtor's bank) when it creates the payment instruction
  • Immutable — it must be passed unchanged through every hop in the payment chain
  • Carried in MT 103 field :121: (in the User Header, block 3) and in ISO 20022 pacs.008 element /UETRId
  • Used as the key to query the GPI Tracker for real-time payment status
// UETR in MT 103 User Header (Block 3) {3:{108:BARCGB22REF0001}{121:eb6305c9-1bed-462e-9797-a52a6b9a2f35}} // UETR in ISO 20022 pacs.008 <GrpHdr> <MsgId>BARCGB22-2024-00001</MsgId> <CreDtTm>2024-11-15T09:30:00Z</CreDtTm> </GrpHdr> <CdtTrfTxInf> <PmtId> <InstrId>BARCGB22-2024-00001-001</InstrId> <EndToEndId>ACME-INV2024-0099</EndToEndId> <UETR>eb6305c9-1bed-462e-9797-a52a6b9a2f35</UETR> ← immutable across all hops </PmtId> </CdtTrfTxInf>

GPI Components and SLAs

📍

GPI Tracker (g4C)

The SWIFT GPI Tracker (gpi for Corporates — g4C) is a central cloud-based database where every bank in a payment chain updates the payment status in real time. Status codes: ACSP (accepted and processing), ACCC (confirmed credited to beneficiary account). Banks can query the tracker using the UETR. Corporates access the tracker through their banks' portals.

✅

Universal Confirmation

Every GPI bank that credits the beneficiary account MUST send a confirmation back to the originating bank via the GPI Tracker. The confirmation must include: the exact amount credited, the currency, the credit timestamp, and the exchange rate applied (if any). This enables the ordering bank and corporate to confirm that funds reached the beneficiary account.

🛡️

Payment Controls

Real-time fraud and sanctions screening applied at the SWIFT infrastructure level before message delivery. Payment Controls can block or flag suspicious messages before they reach the recipient bank — adding an additional screening layer on top of the receiving bank's own controls.

⚙️

Transaction Manager

SWIFT's Transaction Manager is an orchestration layer that manages the full payment lifecycle end-to-end. It stores the payment instruction, tracks status updates, manages exceptions and repair, and will eventually become the central hub for all SWIFT cross-border payment flows — moving from a store-and-forward model to a platform model.

GPI SLA: SWIFT GPI mandates that banks credit the beneficiary account or pass the payment to the next bank on the same day (in the beneficiary's time zone) in most cases. Banks that breach GPI SLAs are reported in the GPI performance dashboards visible to all GPI member banks. This creates commercial pressure to maintain high performance standards.

5.7 R-Transactions

R-transactions are payment-related messages that move funds in the opposite direction to (or cancel) the original payment. "R" stands for Reject, Return, Recall, Reversal, Refusal, or Revocation — each representing a distinct scenario with specific messages, timing rules, and initiating parties.

R-Transaction Decision Flow

1
Payment submitted to clearing — pacs.008 or pain.001 sent
2
Pre-settlement? If yes → Reject (pacs.002 RJCT) or Revocation (pain.007 if still in initiation) is possible. No funds at risk.
3
Post-settlement — can it be applied? If creditor bank cannot apply: Return (pacs.004). Funds move back through the chain.
4
Post-settlement — originator wants it back? Recall request: camt.056. Creditor bank investigates.
5
Recall accepted? If yes: pacs.004 return initiated. If no: camt.029 Refusal sent. Originator must pursue other recovery.

5.8 Exception and Investigation Messages

Beyond R-transactions, the ISO 20022 camt family includes a set of exception and investigation messages used when there are problems with a payment that require clarification, correction, or resolution between banks. These messages are critical for payment operations teams handling non-straight-through items.

Message IDMessage NameMT EquivalentPurpose & When to Use
camt.026UnableToApplyMT 110/196 (informal)Sent by the creditor bank to the previous agent in the chain when it has received the funds but cannot apply them to a beneficiary account due to missing or incorrect data in the payment instruction. Examples: beneficiary account number is missing, name does not match the account, payment purpose code is mandatory for the destination country and was not provided. The camt.026 asks the previous bank to provide the missing information. It does NOT return the funds — it holds them pending resolution. Common reason codes: MM20 (missing creditor name), MM21 (missing creditor address), MM22 (wrong account number).
camt.027ClaimNonReceiptMT 110/196 (informal)Sent by a bank that expects to receive a payment but has not received it within the expected timeframe. The claimant bank sends camt.027 to the previous agent in the chain, stating the expected payment details and requesting investigation. Triggers: a bank has not received expected funds by value date; a beneficiary customer has complained that they have not received a payment. The investigated bank must respond promptly — either confirming the payment was sent (with proof) or initiating a recall/investigation.
camt.028AdditionalPaymentInformationMT 196 response (informal)The response message to a camt.026 (Unable to Apply). Sent by the bank that received the camt.026 inquiry, providing the additional or corrected payment information that will enable the creditor bank to apply the funds. May contain: corrected beneficiary name, correct account number, additional address details, purpose code, or regulatory information. Once the creditor bank receives camt.028 with sufficient data, it can apply the payment and close the investigation.
camt.029ResolutionOfInvestigationMT 196 resolution (informal)The generic investigation resolution message. Sent in response to: (1) a camt.056 recall request — either positive (funds will be returned via pacs.004) or negative (Refusal); (2) a camt.027 claim non-receipt — confirming receipt status; (3) other investigation messages. For a positive resolution of a recall, camt.029 precedes or accompanies the pacs.004 return. For a negative resolution (Refusal), camt.029 carries reason codes explaining why the recall was refused (e.g., LEGL — legal reasons, CUST — customer dispute, AC03 — incorrect beneficiary account).
camt.087RequestToModifyPaymentMT 195 (informal)Sent by the originating bank or any bank in the chain to request a modification to an in-flight payment that has already been submitted but may not yet have reached the creditor bank. Examples: changing the beneficiary account number, updating the beneficiary name, adding a missing purpose code, correcting the beneficiary address. The receiving bank may accept and action the modification, or reject it if the payment has already been processed. camt.087 is used when a full recall and re-send would be disproportionate for a minor data correction.

Investigation Message Workflow

1
Problem identified — creditor bank receives payment but cannot apply (missing data) → sends camt.026 to previous agent
2
Previous agent receives camt.026 — investigates, gathers missing data → responds with camt.028 (Additional Payment Information)
3
If data received — creditor bank applies funds; investigation closed. If data still insufficient → creditor bank returns funds via pacs.004
4
Non-receipt suspected — bank sends camt.027 (Claim Non-Receipt). Investigated bank responds via camt.029 with resolution (payment found, or recall initiated)
5
Amendment needed in-flight — send camt.087 (Request to Modify Payment) to the next bank holding the payment
GPI and Investigations: SWIFT GPI has dramatically reduced the volume of investigation messages. With end-to-end tracking via the UETR, banks and corporates can see in real time where a payment is. Most "where is my payment?" queries that previously generated camt.027 (Claim Non-Receipt) messages can now be resolved by checking the GPI Tracker directly. However, exceptions relating to data quality (camt.026/028) and recalls (camt.056/029) remain common and are part of every payment operations team's daily workflow.
