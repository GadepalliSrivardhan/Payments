Lynx RTGS System — Canada

Overview of Lynx — Canada's ISO 20022-native RTGS replacing LVTS, payment processing, liquidity management, and the four settlement mechanisms: UPM, LSM, RCM and RTM.

LynxPayments CanadaISO 20022UPMLSMRCMRTMBank of CanadaLVTS

11.1 Overview of Lynx

Lynx is Canada's high-value payment system (HVPS), operated by Payments Canada under the oversight of the Bank of Canada. Lynx launched on 19 August 2021, replacing the legacy LVTS (Large Value Transfer System) that had operated since 1999. Lynx is Canada's first ISO 20022-native RTGS system, designed to be more resilient, efficient, and interoperable with global payment standards.

🍁

Operator

Payments Canada — the national payments infrastructure organisation. Settlement occurs in Bank of Canada accounts (ESAs — Exchange Settlement Accounts).

💵

Daily Volume

Approximately CAD 200 billion daily across ~30,000–40,000 transactions. Handles systemically important Canadian dollar payments.

📋

ISO 20022 Native

Lynx was built from the ground up to use ISO 20022 messaging (pacs.008, pacs.009, camt.052, camt.053). No legacy MT format support.

🔒

Collateral-Free

Unlike LVTS which required pledged collateral, Lynx uses a pure prefunding model — participants must hold positive balances in their Bank of Canada ESAs at all times.

Why Lynx Replaced LVTS

The LVTS (Large Value Transfer System) was a deferred net settlement system with collateral requirements. While effective, it was built on aging technology, supported only proprietary messaging formats, and was not easily adaptable to ISO 20022 or modern liquidity management needs. Lynx was designed to address these limitations with a modern, flexible architecture and native ISO 20022 support — positioning Canada's payment infrastructure for future interoperability with global systems.

LVTS vs Lynx: LVTS operated as a DNS with collateral-backed credit limits. Lynx operates as a true RTGS with prefunding — each payment requires a positive balance in the sender's ESA. This eliminates credit risk while requiring participants to manage intraday liquidity more actively.

11.2 Lynx Payments Processing

Direct Participants

Direct participants in Lynx must be eligible financial institutions — primarily Schedule I and Schedule II banks, and credit unions with Bank of Canada ESAs. As of launch, Lynx has approximately 15 direct participants. Smaller financial institutions access Lynx indirectly through a direct participant (similar to correspondent banking). Each direct participant must hold an Exchange Settlement Account (ESA) at the Bank of Canada, which serves as the settlement account for all Lynx transactions.

ISO 20022 Messaging in Lynx

All Lynx messages use ISO 20022 XML. Key messages:

Payment Finality

Lynx provides immediate, irrevocable finality for each settled payment. Once a payment is processed by Lynx and the sending participant's ESA is debited, settlement is final under the Payment Clearing and Settlement Act (PCSA). The PCSA provides statutory finality — settled payments cannot be reversed or set aside by insolvency proceedings. This legal finality is critical for the systemic safety of Canada's payment infrastructure.

11.3 Lynx Transaction Lifecycle Flow

The following is the end-to-end flow of a pacs.009 (Financial Institution Credit Transfer) payment through Lynx, from origination at the sending bank to final credit at the receiving bank. The flow illustrates how the mechanism assignment (UPM, LSM, or RCM) determines the settlement path.

1

Payment Origination — pacs.009 Created

The sending bank's payment operations system generates an ISO 20022 pacs.009 (Financial Institution Credit Transfer) message. The message includes: instructing agent BIC, instructed agent BIC, amount, value date, settlement method, and all required structured remittance data. For customer-originated payments, a pacs.008 may be used instead.

2

Priority Assignment — UPM, LSM, or RCM

Before submission, the sending bank assigns the payment to one of three priority mechanisms: UPM (Urgent Payment Mechanism) for time-critical, immediate-gross-settlement payments; LSM (Liquidity Saving Mechanism) for standard large-value payments where bilateral/multilateral offset is desired; or RCM (Regular Credit Mechanism) for standard-priority payments processed FIFO. This assignment is embedded in the pacs.009 message header.

3

Submission to Lynx via Payments Canada Network

The sending bank's system transmits the pacs.009 to Lynx over Payments Canada's secure network infrastructure. Connectivity uses SWIFTNet or a direct Payments Canada-approved connection. The message is received by the Lynx gateway for pre-processing.

4

Lynx Validation

Lynx performs a suite of validations before accepting the payment: ISO 20022 schema validation (message must conform to the Lynx usage guidelines); BIC validity check (instructed agent BIC must be a Lynx direct participant); sender ESA balance check — for UPM and RCM, Lynx immediately verifies the sender's ESA has sufficient positive balance to cover the payment. For LSM, the balance check is deferred to the offset matching step. If any validation fails, Lynx immediately returns a pacs.002 with status RJCT and the relevant reason code.

5

UPM Path — Immediate Gross Settlement

For payments assigned to UPM: Lynx processes immediately and individually. The ESA of the sending bank is debited in real time; the ESA of the receiving bank is credited simultaneously. Settlement is FINAL and irrevocable under the PCSA the moment the ESA entries are made. No queuing, no netting, no waiting. Lynx sends a pacs.002 with status ACCP (Accepted and Completed) to the sending bank, confirming settlement.

6

LSM Path — Bilateral/Multilateral Offset

For payments assigned to LSM: the payment enters the LSM queue. The Lynx optimisation algorithm continuously scans the LSM queue for bilateral offsets (Bank A owes Bank B CAD 50M and Bank B owes Bank A CAD 45M — both can settle simultaneously with only CAD 5M net liquidity) and multilateral offset cycles (Bank A → Bank B → Bank C → Bank A). When a viable offset is identified, all matched payments settle simultaneously — ESAs are debited and credited for the net difference. This dramatically reduces gross liquidity consumption while achieving the same settlement outcome.

7

Settlement Confirmation — pacs.002 Sent

After settlement (via UPM or LSM or RCM), Lynx generates and sends a pacs.002 (Payment Status Report) to the sending bank: status ACCP (Accepted and Completed / settled) for successful settlement; status RJCT (Rejected) with an ISO 20022 reason code (e.g., AM04 — insufficient funds, RC01 — invalid BIC, DT01 — invalid value date) for failures. The pacs.002 is the definitive settlement confirmation and triggers downstream accounting entries at the sending bank.

8

Debit/Credit Notifications — camt.054 Sent to Both Banks

Lynx sends camt.054 (Bank to Customer Debit/Credit Notification) messages to both the sending bank (debit notification confirming ESA was debited) and the receiving bank (credit notification confirming ESA was credited). These notifications allow both banks to update their internal nostro/vostro position records and reconcile their intraday liquidity positions in real time.

9

Receiving Bank Credits Beneficiary

Upon receipt of the camt.054 credit notification, the receiving bank's straight-through processing (STP) system identifies the underlying beneficiary from the original payment details in the pacs.009 (passed through from step 1) and credits the beneficiary's account in the bank's core banking system. For interbank payments (FI-to-FI), the counterpart bank records the settlement in its treasury/nostro systems. The payment is now fully complete from origination to final credit.

Message Chain Summary: pacs.009 (origination) → pacs.002 ACCP/RJCT (Lynx settlement status) → camt.054 (debit/credit notifications to both banks). For customer payments using pacs.008, the same flow applies with pacs.008 substituted at step 1.

11.4 Lynx Operating Hours and Schedule

Lynx operates on a defined business day schedule aligned with Canadian financial market hours. Unusually, the Lynx business day begins the previous calendar evening — the system opens at 9:00 PM ET (Eastern Time) the night before, after the overnight maintenance window closes, and accepts payments through to close the following business day evening.

Key Operational Note: The Lynx business day begins at 9:00 PM ET the previous evening — meaning a payment submitted at 10:00 PM on a Monday evening is processed on Tuesday's Lynx business day. This early open enables banks to pre-position payments and manage overnight liquidity needs efficiently. The Bank of Canada's Intraday Liquidity Facility (ILF) repo window also aligns with these operating hours.
Holiday Schedule: Lynx does not operate on Canadian federal public holidays. On the last business day before a holiday, Payments Canada may issue an amended schedule with earlier cutoff times to allow for holiday period liquidity management. Participants are notified in advance through Payments Canada's operational communications.

11.5 UPM, LSM, RCM and RTM — The Four Mechanisms

Lynx incorporates four distinct payment processing mechanisms, each designed for different payment priorities and liquidity management strategies. Participants assign payments to mechanisms based on urgency and their desire to benefit from offsetting/netting efficiency.

1. UPM — Urgent Payment Mechanism

UPM is the highest-priority queue in Lynx. Payments assigned to UPM are settled immediately and individually on a gross basis as soon as sufficient funds are available in the sending participant's ESA. UPM provides the fastest settlement — equivalent to pure RTGS with no queueing or netting. Used for: time-critical, high-value payments where immediacy is paramount (e.g., money market transactions with same-day settlement deadlines, urgent corporate payments).

2. LSM — Liquidity Saving Mechanism

LSM is the liquidity-efficient queue. Payments placed in LSM are held and processed by the Lynx algorithm which continuously scans for bilateral and multilateral offsets — pairs or cycles of payments between participants that can be settled simultaneously using only the net difference in liquidity. LSM significantly reduces the gross liquidity consumed. It is analogous to the queue-based netting found in systems like CHIPS and the ECB's TARGET2. Most standard-priority large-value payments are submitted to LSM to benefit from its liquidity saving properties.

3. RCM — Regular Credit Mechanism

RCM is the standard, lower-priority queue. Payments in RCM are processed in FIFO (First In, First Out) order, on a gross basis, as ESA funds become available. RCM sits between UPM (immediate) and LSM (optimised) in terms of priority. Banks may use RCM for payments that are important but don't need the immediacy of UPM and where the volume is insufficient to benefit significantly from LSM netting. RCM ensures all queued payments are eventually settled as liquidity flows in.

4. RTM — Real-Time Mechanism

RTM supports retail payment system (RPS) settlement — the settlement of net positions from ancillary systems. When Canada's retail payment systems (like the Automated Clearing Settlement System, ACSS) calculate their end-of-cycle net positions, these positions are settled in Lynx via the RTM. RTM handles both bilateral net positions and multilateral net positions submitted by clearing agents. It provides a clean interface between Lynx and ancillary systems, ensuring that retail payment settlement flows efficiently through the RTGS infrastructure.

11.6 UPM/LSM/RCM Queue Interaction and Re-Assignment

Understanding how payments interact between queues, and how banks can manage queue assignments, is essential for Lynx liquidity operators. The following covers re-assignment capabilities, monitoring tools, and end-of-day handling for unmatched LSM payments.

Can Payments Be Re-Assigned Between Queues?

Yes — a bank can manually re-prioritise a queued payment from LSM to UPM via Lynx's U2A (User-to-Application) interface if the urgency of a payment increases after submission. Scenarios where this applies: a customer complaint about a delayed payment; a regulatory deadline requiring same-day settlement; a money market transaction where counterparty requests urgent settlement; or an unexpected intraday liquidity event requiring the receiving bank to have funds immediately.

Important: Escalation from LSM to UPM is not automatic in Lynx — it requires a manual operator action by a bank's intraday liquidity management team through the Lynx U2A interface. Lynx does not have an automatic escalation rule that upgrades LSM payments based on time elapsed or other criteria. Banks must monitor their queues actively and intervene when needed.

Queue Monitoring and Liquidity Dashboards

Lynx provides participating banks with real-time liquidity dashboards through the Lynx U2A interface. These dashboards display:

  • Queue depth — the number and value of payments currently held in each of the bank's UPM, LSM, and RCM queues
  • ESA balance — the bank's current Exchange Settlement Account balance at the Bank of Canada, updated in real time after each settlement
  • Projected end-of-day position — an estimate of the bank's ESA balance at close, based on known inflows (expected incoming payments) and outflows (queued outgoing payments)
  • Matched vs unmatched LSM payments — visibility into which LSM payments are awaiting an offsetting payment vs. those that are actively in matching cycles

These tools allow treasury and liquidity management teams to make informed decisions about re-prioritisation, additional funding from the Bank of Canada's Intraday Liquidity Facility, or other intraday liquidity actions.

Unmatched LSM Payments at End of Day

Payments submitted to LSM that have not found an offset by the system-to-system cutoff (6:00 PM ET) are handled as follows:

A

Offsetting Payment Arrives

If an offsetting payment arrives before the cutoff, LSM matching triggers and the payment settles normally. This is the intended LSM outcome.

B

Bank Manually Upgrades to UPM

The sending bank uses the U2A interface to re-assign the unmatched LSM payment to UPM. Lynx then immediately attempts gross settlement against the bank's ESA balance. If sufficient funds exist, the payment settles immediately.

C

End-of-Day Escalation to RCM / Return

Unmatched LSM payments that remain in queue at close may be escalated to RCM for final gross settlement attempts using remaining ESA balances, or — if ESA balance is insufficient — returned to the sending bank with a pacs.002 RJCT status. The sending bank must then resubmit the payment the following business day or make alternative arrangements.

RTM Interaction with UPM/LSM/RCM

The RTM (Real-Time Mechanism) operates as a separate and independent settlement process within Lynx for ancillary system net position settlement. RTM does not interact with the UPM, LSM, or RCM queues used for individual payment processing. When ACSS submits a net settlement file via RTM, those net positions settle directly in bank ESAs through the RTM process — not through the individual payment queues. This clean separation ensures that retail payment batch settlement does not create queue congestion or liquidity conflicts with individual high-value payments.

11.7 RTM — Ancillary System Settlement Mechanics

The RTM (Real-Time Mechanism) is the interface through which ancillary payment systems submit their net settlement positions to Lynx for settlement in Bank of Canada ESAs. The most important ancillary system using RTM is ACSS (Automated Clearing Settlement System) — Canada's retail clearing system for cheques, AFT (Automated Funds Transfers), and Interac transactions.

How ACSS Submits to Lynx via RTM — Step by Step

1

ACSS Clearing Cycle Completes

The ACSS clearing cycle processes all retail payment items — cheques, AFT credit/debit transactions, Interac e-Transfer settlements, and image-based items — submitted by direct clearers during the clearing period. ACSS has typically 3 clearing cycles per business day. Each cycle closes at a defined time and triggers net position calculation.

2

ACSS Calculates Multilateral Net Positions

After each clearing cycle closes, ACSS calculates the multilateral net positions for all direct clearers — the net of all items exchanged between all pairs of participants, resulting in a single net debit or net credit position for each direct clearer in the cycle. This is the fundamental efficiency of net settlement — hundreds of thousands of individual transactions are reduced to a single net position per participant.

3

ACSS Submits Net Settlement File to Lynx RTM

ACSS (operated by Payments Canada) submits the net settlement file to Lynx through the RTM interface. The file specifies the net debit/credit obligation of each direct clearer. The file is formatted as a structured Lynx RTM settlement instruction.

4

RTM Validates the Settlement File

Lynx RTM performs validation on the submitted file: Balanced check — total debits must equal total credits (the file must be mathematically balanced); Participant eligibility — all participants listed must be active Lynx direct participants; ESA balance check — each net-debit participant must have sufficient positive balance in their ESA to cover their net debit obligation. If all validations pass, Lynx proceeds to settlement. If validation fails, the entire file is rejected and ACSS is notified.

5

Simultaneous Multi-Party Settlement

If all ESAs have sufficient balances, Lynx RTM executes a simultaneous multi-party settlement: all net-debit participants' ESAs are debited and all net-credit participants' ESAs are credited in a single atomic operation. This is not a series of individual payments — it is a single balanced settlement event. The settlement is final and irrevocable under the PCSA.

6

Insufficient ESA — Gridlock Resolution

If any net-debit participant's ESA has insufficient balance, Lynx RTM holds the settlement file. The participant is notified and must fund their ESA (via the Bank of Canada's Intraday Liquidity Facility or incoming Lynx receipts) before RTM will retry. If the shortfall persists to close of business, gridlock resolution mechanisms may apply — in extreme cases, the Bank of Canada may provide emergency liquidity to prevent systemic settlement failure.

7

RTM Sends Settlement Confirmation to ACSS

After successful settlement, Lynx RTM sends a settlement confirmation message back to ACSS, confirming that the net positions have been settled in the ESAs. This confirmation is the trigger that allows ACSS to release its own settlement records.

8

ACSS Releases Confirmation to All Direct Clearers

ACSS distributes individual settlement confirmations to all direct clearers, informing them of their settled net position. Direct clearers then update their internal systems, credit or debit their indirect clearer correspondents' accounts, and release funds to end beneficiaries. The retail payment cycle is now fully settled in central bank money.

ACSS Settlement Cycles

ACSS typically runs 3 settlement cycles per business day. The final ACSS settlement cycle occurs in the afternoon — direct clearers are required to have their net debit positions fully funded in their ESAs by a specific time (notified by Payments Canada) to ensure the final cycle settles before Lynx closes at 6:30 PM ET. Failure to fund a net debit position in the final ACSS cycle is a serious operational and regulatory event.

Why RTM Matters: Every Interac e-Transfer, cheque deposit, and pre-authorised debit payment in Canada ultimately settles in central bank money (Bank of Canada ESAs) via RTM. Lynx's RTM is therefore the foundation of all retail payment finality in Canada, even for transactions that originate in consumer-facing apps and platforms.

11.8 How RTR Will Interact with Lynx

Canada's Real-Time Rail (RTR) is a next-generation instant payment system currently under development by Payments Canada. RTR is designed to provide 24/7 real-time credit transfers with immediate finality for consumers and businesses — similar to FedNow (USA), Faster Payments (UK), and TIPS (Eurozone). Understanding how RTR will integrate with Lynx is important for understanding Canada's future payment architecture.

RTR Overview

⚡

RTR Purpose

24/7 real-time credit transfers with immediate, irrevocable finality for end users. Target: confirmation of payment in under 60 seconds, 24 hours a day, 365 days a year.

📋

ISO 20022 Native

RTR will be built natively on ISO 20022 messaging, consistent with Lynx and global instant payment best practices. This ensures interoperability with Lynx and future cross-border payment systems.

🏦

Prefunded Settlement Model

RTR will use a prefunded settlement model — similar to the ECB's TIPS (TARGET Instant Payment Settlement). Banks will fund a dedicated RTR settlement account before being able to send RTR payments.

🔗

Lynx as Backbone

Even with RTR operating 24/7, Lynx remains the ultimate settlement backbone. RTR net positions will settle via Lynx RTM — similar to how ACSS settles today.

RTR Settlement Architecture

Prefunded RTR Settlement Account

Prefunding Model: Each bank participating in RTR will be required to maintain a dedicated RTR Settlement Account prefunded with CAD balances. Every RTR payment sent will immediately debit the sender's RTR Settlement Account and credit the receiver's RTR Settlement Account. This provides instantaneous gross settlement for each RTR payment without requiring real-time access to Lynx (which has daily operating hours). The prefunded accounts act as a liquidity buffer enabling 24/7 operation.

End-of-Day Lynx Reconciliation

Lynx RTM Settlement: At the end of each Lynx business day (or at defined intervals), the net positions accumulated in the RTR Settlement Accounts will be settled via Lynx RTM — debiting and crediting bank ESAs at the Bank of Canada for the net RTR activity. This mirrors exactly how ACSS retail positions settle via RTM today. Lynx remains the mechanism for final settlement in central bank money.

Path to RTR Launch

RTR's target launch was originally planned for 2024–2025, but the programme has faced significant delays due to: the technical complexity of implementing ISO 20022 natively across all systems; participant bank readiness and internal technology upgrade requirements; governance and rulebook development; and the broader Payments Canada modernisation programme sequencing. Payments Canada continues to work with industry stakeholders on implementation. When launched, RTR will complete Canada's modernised payment ecosystem — with Lynx at the top for high-value, ACSS (modernised) for batch retail, and RTR for instant retail payments, all interconnected through ISO 20022 and Lynx settlement.

Lynx as the Enduring Settlement Foundation: A key architectural principle of Canada's payment modernisation is that Lynx remains the ultimate settlement backbone for all payment systems — including the new RTR. Just as ACSS net positions settle in Bank of Canada ESAs via Lynx RTM, RTR net positions will also flow through Lynx for final central bank money settlement. This ensures Canada's entire payment infrastructure — from instant retail to high-value interbank — ultimately settles in risk-free central bank money.

11.9 Canadian Payments Ecosystem

Lynx sits at the top of Canada's payment hierarchy. Below it are several retail and mass payment systems:

⚡

Interac e-Transfer

Canada's dominant consumer real-time payment service. Uses email/mobile as proxies for account lookup. Operated by Interac Corp. ~$250 billion transferred annually. Settlement via ACSS.

🔄

AFT — Automated Funds Transfer

Canada's ACH equivalent — batch credit and debit transfers. Used for payroll, pre-authorised debits (utility bills, mortgage payments). Governed by Payments Canada Rule E2.

📄

ACSS — Automated Clearing Settlement System

Clears retail items (AFT, cheques, image-based items). Net positions from ACSS settle in Lynx via RTM each business day. Replacing with ACSS Modernisation project.

🚀

RTR — Real-Time Rail (Upcoming)

Canada's planned instant payment system (equivalent to RTP/FedNow in US). Will provide 24/7 real-time credit transfers with immediate finality. Under development by Payments Canada.

Payment Canada's Modernisation Programme: Alongside Lynx, Payments Canada is modernising the entire Canadian payments ecosystem — upgrading ACSS, building the RTR (Real-Time Rail), and implementing ISO 20022 across all systems. Canada's approach emphasises safety and stakeholder consultation, with phased implementation over several years.
