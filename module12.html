12.1 China Payments Overview

China operates one of the world's most sophisticated and highest-volume payment ecosystems. The People's Bank of China (PBOC) is the central bank and primary regulator, overseeing both domestic payment systems and the cross-border RMB infrastructure. China's payment systems are structured in layers:

🏛️

PBOC — Regulator

People's Bank of China oversees all payment systems, issues Payment Business Licences, and operates CNAPS. Sets rules for domestic and cross-border RMB payment systems.

🔗

CNAPS

China National Advanced Payment System — the overarching infrastructure comprising HVPS (high-value) and BEPS (batch/retail). Operated by PBOC/CNAPS operations centre.

💳

UnionPay

China's domestic card network — mandatory for all bank cards issued in China. Also has international reach. Operated by China UnionPay, a state-backed banking association.

📱

Alipay & WeChat Pay

Dominant consumer payment platforms. Regulated since 2016 under PBOC's non-bank payment supervision. Required to hold all client funds in PBOC's Centralised Depository.

CNAPS Architecture

CNAPS (China National Advanced Payment System) is the umbrella infrastructure for China's interbank payments. It has two main components: HVPS (High Value Payment System) — the RTGS for large-value and urgent CNY payments, processing all transactions individually and immediately; and BEPS (Bulk Electronic Payment System) — China's ACH equivalent, processing retail batch payments in scheduled cycles. CNAPS is operated by the PBOC and settles in PBOC reserve accounts. Major Chinese banks are direct participants; smaller banks access via their correspondent banks.

12.2 CDFCPS — China Domestic Foreign Currency Payment System

The CDFCPS (China Domestic Foreign Currency Payment System) — also referred to as FCDPS — is operated by the PBOC and enables interbank settlement of foreign currency transactions within China. Before CDFCPS, banks in China had to route domestic foreign currency interbank transfers through overseas correspondent banks, creating inefficiency and delay.

Currencies Supported

CDFCPS supports major foreign currencies used in China's international trade and finance, including:

  • USD (US Dollar) — the dominant currency in CDFCPS by volume
  • EUR (Euro)
  • HKD (Hong Kong Dollar)
  • JPY (Japanese Yen)
  • GBP (British Pound)
  • AUD, CAD, SGD and other currencies with sufficient transaction volume

How CDFCPS Works

CDFCPS connects domestic Chinese banks and enables them to transfer foreign currency funds between their foreign currency reserve accounts held at the PBOC or at designated settlement banks. Settlement is in central bank money (PBOC accounts) in the respective foreign currency. This provides domestic China with an efficient, on-shore settlement mechanism for foreign currency interbank transactions, reducing the need for offshore correspondent arrangements for purely domestic foreign currency transfers.

Significance: CDFCPS is critical for China's FX trading market — when Chinese banks trade USD/CNY or EUR/CNY with each other, CDFCPS handles the foreign currency leg of the settlement, while HVPS handles the CNY leg. This enables domestic PvP settlement for FX transactions within China.

12.3 CIPS — Cross-Border Interbank Payment System

CIPS (Cross-Border Interbank Payment System) is a renminbi (RMB/CNY) cross-border payment and clearing system established by the PBOC, with processing operations run by CIPS Co. Ltd (a state-backed company). CIPS launched its Phase 1 in October 2015 and Phase 2 (with real-time RTGS capability) in May 2018. CIPS is a cornerstone of China's strategy to internationalise the RMB and reduce dependence on SWIFT and US dollar correspondent banking for RMB transactions.

🌐

Scope

Cross-border RMB transactions — trade finance, FDI settlements, securities investment, interbank transfers. Used across 100+ countries and regions.

🏦

Participants

Direct Participants (~70–80 banks) hold CIPS accounts and connect directly. Indirect Participants (~1,200+) access CIPS through a direct participant relationship.

💱

Annual Volume

Over CNY 100 trillion (~$14 trillion) in annual transaction value as of recent years, growing rapidly with RMB internationalisation.

📡

Messaging

CIPS uses both SWIFT messaging (SWIFTNet FIN and now ISO 20022) and the CIPS proprietary messaging standard — giving participants flexibility.

CIPS Operating Model

CIPS operates on a hybrid settlement model: it provides real-time gross settlement (RTGS) for high-value, time-critical transactions during business hours, and end-of-day net settlement for lower-value transactions. CIPS operating window spans approximately 5:00 AM to 11:00 PM Beijing Time (CST) — an extended window designed to overlap with major financial market time zones in Asia, Europe, and the US.

12.4 CHATS — Clearing House Automated Transfer System (Hong Kong)

CHATS (Clearing House Automated Transfer System) is Hong Kong's real-time gross settlement system, operated by HKICL (Hong Kong Interbank Clearing Limited) under the oversight of the HKMA (Hong Kong Monetary Authority). CHATS is unique globally in operating four parallel RTGS systems — one for each major currency transacted in Hong Kong — within a single integrated platform.

Four RTGS Systems in CHATS

🇭🇰

HKD RTGS

Settlement in Hong Kong Dollars. Settled in HKMA's clearing accounts. Handles domestic HKD interbank payments and equity market settlement (DvP with HKEX).

🇺🇸

USD RTGS

USD real-time gross settlement in Hong Kong. Settlement through HSBC Hong Kong as the settlement institution (not Federal Reserve). Unique globally — offshore USD RTGS in central bank equivalent money.

🇪🇺

EUR RTGS

Euro RTGS in Hong Kong. Settlement through Deutsche Bank AG (Hong Kong) as settlement institution. Enables euro payments to be settled locally in HK without routing via TARGET2.

🇨🇳

RMB RTGS

Renminbi RTGS — Hong Kong is the world's largest offshore RMB settlement centre. Settlement through the Bank of China (HK) as the RMB settlement bank appointed by the PBOC. Connected to CIPS.

CHATS Architecture and Operating Hours

CHATS provides a unified technical platform (operated by HKICL) with separate liquidity pools for each currency. Banks in Hong Kong can participate in one or more of the four RTGS systems depending on their business needs. The systems operate during defined windows that align with the major financial centres trading hours for each currency. CHATS connects to SWIFT for cross-border messaging, and the RMB RTGS maintains direct linkage with CIPS for mainland China payments.

12.5 HVPS+ and ISO 20022 in China and Hong Kong

HVPS+ Usage Guidelines

HVPS+ (High Value Payments Plus) is the ISO 20022 usage guideline set developed by the Payment Market Practice Group (PMPG) and adopted by many RTGS systems globally when migrating to ISO 20022. HVPS+ defines the specific subset of ISO 20022 fields, business rules, and message usage conventions applicable to high-value payment systems (RTGS). Both China's HVPS and Hong Kong's CHATS have considered HVPS+ in their ISO 20022 migration strategies.

China's ISO 20022 Journey

China has been an early and active adopter of ISO 20022. CIPS Phase 2 (2018) introduced ISO 20022 messaging support alongside the proprietary CIPS format. CNAPS/HVPS is undergoing ISO 20022 migration — the PBOC has published ISO 20022 migration roadmaps for both HVPS and BEPS. China's implementation follows HVPS+ guidelines adapted for Chinese market requirements, with additional fields for Chinese regulatory reporting (e.g., SAFE reporting for cross-border flows).

Hong Kong's ISO 20022 Adoption

The HKMA has mandated ISO 20022 adoption for CHATS. The HKD RTGS completed ISO 20022 migration in recent years. The RMB RTGS uses ISO 20022 natively due to its CIPS integration. USD and EUR RTGS systems are following the global migration timeline. Hong Kong's adoption aligns with HVPS+ guidelines and SWIFT CBPR+ for cross-border messages, ensuring seamless interoperability with other major financial centres.

Hong Kong as a Global Gateway: CHATS's multi-currency RTGS capability makes Hong Kong a unique node in global payment infrastructure. A bank in Hong Kong can settle USD, EUR, RMB, and HKD transactions all within a single local system — eliminating the need for foreign correspondents for those currencies. This is particularly valuable for trade finance and FX settlement in the Asia-Pacific time zone.
