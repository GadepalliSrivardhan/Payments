12.1 China Domestic Payment Infrastructure — CNAPS, HVPS, BEPS

China operates one of the world's largest and most sophisticated payment ecosystems. The People's Bank of China (PBOC) is the central bank and primary regulator, overseeing both domestic payment systems and cross-border RMB infrastructure. The core domestic interbank infrastructure is CNAPS (China National Advanced Payment System).

🏛️

HVPS — High Value Payment System

China's RTGS for large-value and urgent CNY payments. Settles transactions individually and immediately in PBOC reserve accounts. ~1,500 direct participants. Operates 8:30 AM–5:00 PM Beijing time.

📦

BEPS — Bulk Electronic Payment System

China's ACH equivalent for retail batch payments. Processes payroll, supplier payments, consumer debits. Three sessions per day. T+0 same-day settlement for most transactions. Covers hundreds of millions of daily transactions.

🌐

IBPS — Internet Banking Payment System

Online banking payment infrastructure. Enables real-time retail transfers through internet/mobile banking channels. Widely used by consumers and small businesses for daily payments.

📱

Alipay & WeChat Pay

Dominant consumer payment platforms regulated by PBOC. Required to hold all client funds in PBOC's Centralised Depository (since 2017). Processed billions of transactions daily at peak.

HVPS Transaction Flow

1

Payment Submission

Sending bank submits payment instruction to HVPS via direct PBOC connection (proprietary protocol or CNAPS messaging). Message includes payer/payee details, amount, value date, and purpose code.

2

Validation

HVPS validates: sender authentication, message format compliance, available balance in sending bank's PBOC reserve account, and purpose code validity. Sanctions screening is also applied for applicable transaction types.

3

Real-Time Settlement

HVPS simultaneously debits the sending bank's PBOC reserve account and credits the receiving bank's PBOC reserve account. Settlement is final and irrevocable — RTGS in central bank money.

4

Notification and Crediting

Both banks receive real-time settlement notifications from HVPS. The receiving bank credits the beneficiary customer's account immediately upon receiving the HVPS credit notification.

Alipay and WeChat Pay — Regulatory Framework

Alipay (Ant Group) and WeChat Pay (Tencent) transformed China's consumer payments but their rapid growth prompted significant regulatory attention from the PBOC.

12.2 CDFCPS — China Domestic Foreign Currency Payment System

The CDFCPS (China Domestic Foreign Currency Payment System) — also referred to as FCDPS — is operated by the PBOC and enables interbank settlement of foreign currency transactions within China. Before CDFCPS, banks in China had to route domestic foreign currency interbank transfers through overseas correspondent banks, creating inefficiency, delay, and unnecessary offshore exposure.

How CDFCPS Works

CDFCPS connects domestic Chinese banks and enables them to transfer foreign currency funds between their foreign currency reserve accounts at the PBOC or at designated settlement banks. Settlement is in central bank money in the respective foreign currency. Supported currencies include: USD, EUR, HKD, JPY, GBP, AUD, CAD, SGD and others with sufficient domestic transaction volume.

PvP Significance: CDFCPS is critical for China's FX trading market. When Chinese banks trade USD/CNY or EUR/CNY with each other domestically, CDFCPS handles the foreign currency leg of settlement while HVPS handles the CNY leg. This enables a form of domestic PvP settlement for FX within China — eliminating settlement risk for domestic interbank FX transactions without needing CLS Bank.

12.3 CIPS — Cross-Border Interbank Payment System

CIPS launched Phase 1 in October 2015 and Phase 2 (with real-time RTGS) in May 2018. Operated by CIPS Co. Ltd (a state-backed company jointly owned by major Chinese banks and PBOC), CIPS is a cornerstone of China's strategy to internationalise the RMB and reduce dependence on US dollar correspondent banking for RMB transactions.

🌐

Scope & Volume

Cross-border RMB payments across 100+ countries. Over CNY 100 trillion (~$14 trillion) annual value. Processes trade finance, FDI settlements, securities investment, and interbank transfers.

🏦

Participants

~70–80 Direct Participants (hold CIPS accounts, connect directly). ~1,300+ Indirect Participants across 100+ countries (access via a direct participant). Growing rapidly with RMB internationalisation.

⏰

Operating Hours

5:00 AM – 11:00 PM Beijing time (extended to overlap European business hours). Longer than most RTGS systems — designed for global RMB settlement reach.

📡

Messaging

Both SWIFT messaging (ISO 20022) AND CIPS proprietary messaging standard. Gives participants flexibility — CIPS can operate entirely outside SWIFT network when using its own messaging.

CIPS Participant Requirements

Direct Participants

Must be financial institutions incorporated in China or overseas branches/subsidiaries with PBOC approval. Must hold a dedicated CIPS settlement account at a designated CIPS agent bank. Require direct technical connection to the CIPS system. Must comply with Chinese AML/KYC and SAFE (State Administration of Foreign Exchange) reporting requirements. Direct participants are responsible for settling their own and their indirect participants' obligations.
Indirect Participants

Access CIPS through a direct participant acting as their settlement agent. Include overseas banks, fund managers, and other financial institutions globally that need to process RMB payments. Submit instructions to their direct participant, which routes them through CIPS. Do not need a direct CIPS connection or account. Liability for settlement rests with the direct participant.

RMB Internationalisation Strategy and CIPS

CIPS is a key instrument of China's RMB internationalisation policy (人民币国际化):

  • Belt and Road Initiative (BRI): China's global infrastructure investment programme across 150+ countries. BRI projects are increasingly denominated in RMB; CIPS facilitates RMB settlement for BRI transactions, reducing dollar dependency.
  • Offshore RMB Centres: Hong Kong (largest), Singapore, London, Frankfurt, Dubai — all connected to CIPS. These centres enable non-Chinese entities to hold and transact in CNH (offshore RMB).
  • e-CNY + CIPS: China plans to integrate its CBDC (e-CNY/Digital Yuan) with CIPS for cross-border wholesale digital currency payments — creating a new channel for international RMB transactions that operates entirely within Chinese-controlled infrastructure.

12.4 CHATS — Clearing House Automated Transfer System (Hong Kong)

CHATS is operated by HKICL (Hong Kong Interbank Clearing Limited) under HKMA oversight. CHATS is globally unique in operating four parallel RTGS systems — HKD, USD, EUR, and RMB — within a single integrated platform. This makes Hong Kong the only financial centre where a bank can settle four major currencies through a single local infrastructure without relying on foreign correspondents for each.

🇭🇰

HKD RTGS

Settlement in Hong Kong Dollars. Settled in HKMA clearing accounts (central bank money). Handles domestic HKD interbank payments and DvP for HK equities via the CHATS-HKEX interface.

🇺🇸

USD RTGS

USD real-time gross settlement in Hong Kong. Settlement through HSBC Hong Kong as settlement institution. Unique globally — offshore USD RTGS operating in Asia time zone without Federal Reserve involvement.

🇪🇺

EUR RTGS

Euro RTGS in Hong Kong. Settlement through Deutsche Bank AG Hong Kong as settlement institution. Enables euro payments to settle locally in HK hours without routing via TARGET2/T2 in Europe.

🇨🇳

RMB RTGS

Hong Kong is the world's largest offshore RMB settlement centre. Settlement through Bank of China (HK) as PBOC-appointed settlement bank. Directly linked to CIPS for mainland China payments.

CHATS-HKEX DvP Interface

The HKD RTGS connects to Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing (HKEX) for Delivery versus Payment settlement of Hong Kong equities. When securities are transferred on CCASS (HKEX's Central Clearing and Settlement System), the corresponding HKD payment is simultaneously triggered through CHATS. Neither the securities delivery nor the cash payment can occur independently — eliminating settlement risk for HK equity transactions.

12.5 HVPS+ and ISO 20022 in China and Hong Kong

HVPS+ Usage Guidelines

HVPS+ (High Value Payments Plus) is the ISO 20022 usage guideline developed by the Payment Market Practice Group (PMPG) for RTGS systems globally. It defines the specific subset of ISO 20022 fields, validation rules, and usage conventions applicable to large-value payment systems. Both China's HVPS and Hong Kong's CHATS have incorporated HVPS+ in their ISO 20022 migration strategies.

China's ISO 20022 Journey

China has been an early adopter of ISO 20022. CIPS Phase 2 (2018) introduced ISO 20022 messaging support. CNAPS/HVPS is migrating — the PBOC has published roadmaps for both HVPS and BEPS. China's implementation follows HVPS+ guidelines adapted for local requirements, including additional fields for SAFE (State Administration of Foreign Exchange) regulatory reporting on cross-border flows.

Hong Kong's ISO 20022 Adoption

The HKMA mandated ISO 20022 for CHATS. The HKD RTGS completed ISO 20022 migration and the RMB RTGS uses ISO 20022 natively through CIPS. USD and EUR RTGS systems are following the global migration timeline. Hong Kong's adoption aligns with HVPS+ and SWIFT CBPR+ for cross-border messages, ensuring full interoperability with T2 (Europe), Fedwire (US), and other major systems.
