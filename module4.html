RTGS, Low Value Real Time & Hybrid Payment Systems

Understanding RTGS systems, instant retail payment systems (IMPS, UPI, UK Faster Payments, RTP, FedNow, NPP), proxy-based payments, and hybrid retail payment models.

4.1 Real Time Gross Settlement Systems (Generic)

Understanding RTGS Payment Process

A Real Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) system settles each payment individually and immediately — in real time, on a gross (non-netted) basis — across settlement accounts held at the central bank. The defining characteristics are: real time (no delay), gross (no netting), and central bank money (no credit risk on the settlement asset). Once a payment settles, it is final and irrevocable — the receiving bank can use the funds immediately with certainty.

RTGS systems are designed for large-value, time-critical payments — interbank transactions, corporate treasury payments, property purchases, and securities settlement. They operate during defined business hours (though many are extending to near-24/7) and require participants to maintain sufficient central bank reserves to fund each payment in real time.

RTGS Payment Process Flow — End-to-End

The following is the complete step-by-step flow of a single RTGS payment from customer instruction through to final settlement confirmation:

1

Customer Submits Payment Instruction

The ordering customer submits a payment instruction via online banking, corporate treasury system, or API. The instruction includes beneficiary account details, amount, value date, and payment reference.

2

Bank's Payment Engine Validates the Instruction

The originating bank's payment engine performs a series of automated checks before releasing the payment: Format check — validates account numbers, BIC/IBAN, amount format and currency; Sanctions screening — real-time check of payer and payee names/accounts against OFAC, EU, UN, and domestic watchlists; Duplicate detection — compares against recent payments to identify accidental re-submissions within a configurable time window; Liquidity check — verifies the bank's settlement account at the central bank holds sufficient funds to cover the payment.

3

Bank Formats RTGS Message

The payment engine formats the instruction into the RTGS-required message standard — typically a pacs.009 (Financial Institution Credit Transfer, for bank-to-bank/treasury payments) or pacs.008 (FI to FI Customer Credit Transfer, for customer payments). The message is digitally signed and encrypted before transmission.

4

Message Submitted to RTGS System

The formatted message is submitted to the RTGS infrastructure — for example, Fedwire Funds in the USA, T2 in the Eurozone, or CHAPS in the UK. The message enters the RTGS system's processing queue.

5

RTGS Checks Sender's Settlement Account Balance

The RTGS system checks the sending bank's settlement account (e.g., the Fedwire Master Account or T2 RTGS account) in real time. If the balance is sufficient to cover the payment, processing continues immediately. If not, the payment is held in queue until funds arrive (via incoming payments or intraday credit).

6

Simultaneous Debit and Credit — SETTLEMENT FINAL

If funds are sufficient: the RTGS system simultaneously debits the sender's settlement account and credits the receiver's settlement account. This single atomic operation constitutes final, irrevocable settlement. The payment is now legally complete. The receiving bank can release funds to its customer immediately with full certainty — there is zero credit risk from this point forward.

7

RTGS Sends Settlement Confirmation to Both Banks

The RTGS system sends a settlement confirmation to both the sending bank and the receiving bank. This confirmation includes: settlement timestamp, amount settled, RTGS reference number, and the UETR (Unique End-to-end Transaction Reference) where applicable.

8

Receiving Bank Credits Beneficiary's Account

Upon receiving the settlement confirmation, the beneficiary bank credits the beneficiary customer's account in its own ledger. The beneficiary can now access the funds. In practice, the receiving bank may have pre-credited the customer in anticipation of settlement if it has high confidence in the payment.

9

Both Banks Receive Debit/Credit Notifications

Both the sending and receiving banks receive camt.054 (BankToCustomerDebitCreditNotification) messages confirming the debit or credit to their settlement accounts at the central bank. These are used for nostro account reconciliation and internal ledger updates.

Key Principle: Steps 5 and 6 are atomic — the RTGS system either settles the full amount simultaneously or does nothing. There is no partial settlement. This is the defining feature that eliminates credit risk between banks in RTGS.

Features of RTGS for Improving Processing Efficiency

Modern RTGS systems incorporate sophisticated features to reduce liquidity requirements while maintaining real-time finality:

  • Queue Management: Payments that cannot be settled immediately (due to insufficient funds) are held in a queue. When funds arrive, queued payments are released in order or by priority.
  • Bilateral Offsetting: The system identifies pairs of payments between two banks that can be settled simultaneously, requiring only the net difference in liquidity.
  • Multilateral Offsetting / Gridlock Resolution: Algorithms scan for cycles of payments (A owes B, B owes C, C owes A) that can all settle simultaneously with zero net liquidity.
  • Liquidity Saving Mechanism (LSM): Combines real-time gross settlement with netting efficiency — found in hybrid systems like modern CHAPS and Lynx.
  • Throughput Guidelines: Central banks may set percentage targets for payment throughput (e.g., 50% settled by noon, 75% by 2pm) to encourage smooth liquidity distribution throughout the day and prevent end-of-day gridlock.
  • Priority Queues: Urgent or high-value payments can be assigned higher priority to ensure they settle without waiting behind lower-priority batches.

4.2 Instant Retail Payment Systems

Proxy Payment Initiation Using VPA, QR Code etc.

Traditional bank payments require the sender to know the recipient's full bank account details (IBAN, account number, sort code). Instant payment systems have introduced proxy-based payment initiation — the payer uses a simple, memorable identifier (a proxy) to initiate a payment without knowing the recipient's account details.

📱

VPA (Virtual Payment Address)

Used in India's UPI. Format: name@bankname (e.g., john@hdfc). The proxy maps to an underlying bank account via a central directory service.

📷

QR Code

A QR code encodes payment details (VPA, amount, merchant ID). The payer scans and confirms — widely used for merchant payments in India, Singapore (PayNow), China (Alipay/WeChat Pay).

📞

Mobile Number

Used in Australia (PayID), Singapore (PayNow), and India (IMPS). The mobile number is registered in a central directory and mapped to a bank account.

✉️

Email Address

Australia's NPP PayID supports email addresses as proxies. The email is registered and mapped to a BSB+account number.

Models for Proxy Payment Processing and Translation

When a payer enters a proxy (VPA, mobile number, etc.), the payment system must translate it to actual bank account details. This happens via a Central Directory Service or Proxy Lookup Service: (1) Payer's bank queries the directory with the proxy; (2) Directory returns the mapped IBAN/account number and destination bank BIC; (3) Payment is routed to the correct bank; (4) The proxy-to-account mapping is never exposed to the payer, protecting beneficiary privacy.

P2P vs P2M Proxy Distinction

Proxy-based payments serve two fundamentally different use cases — Person to Person (P2P) and Person to Merchant (P2M) — which differ in technical structure, data requirements, and risk profile:

P2P — Person to Person

  • Both parties are individuals (consumers)
  • Typically small, informal amounts (splitting bills, family transfers)
  • No merchant infrastructure needed
  • Proxy is typically a VPA or mobile number entered manually by sender
  • QR code encodes only the VPA — amount is entered by payer
  • Higher return rate potential (wrong proxy, misdirected payments)
  • Less structured remittance data — often just a free-text note

P2M — Person to Merchant

  • Merchant has a registered VPA or static/dynamic QR code
  • QR code encodes amount + merchant ID + transaction reference — amount is pre-populated
  • May include GST integration (India) — tax invoice reference embedded in payment data
  • Receipt/invoice reference carried in remittance information field
  • Lower return rates — merchant infrastructure validates payment
  • More structured data: merchant category code, order ID, GST number
  • Merchant VPA typically ends in a standardised suffix (e.g., @paytm, @razorpay)
Technical Difference — QR Codes: A P2P QR code contains only the VPA (e.g., "john@hdfc") — the sender enters the amount. A P2M dynamic QR code contains the VPA + pre-set amount + merchant transaction reference + optional GST details, making the entire transaction structured and automatable. Static merchant QR codes contain the VPA and merchant ID without an amount (amount entered by customer). Dynamic QR codes are generated per transaction and include the exact amount.

4.3 Approaches in Clearing and Settlement for Instant Payment Systems

1. Hub Approach — India IMPS, UK Faster Payments

In the hub model, a central infrastructure (hub) connects all participating banks. The hub acts as the central counterparty for clearing — each bank connects to the hub, not to every other bank. This reduces the number of bilateral connections from N×(N-1) to just N. India's IMPS (Immediate Payment Service, operated by NPCI) and UK's Faster Payments (operated by Pay.UK) use this model. The hub validates the payment, routes it to the destination bank, and provides settlement certainty. Settlement is typically in periodic cycles (net settlement via central bank) rather than real-time gross.

2. RTGS Approach — SEPA TIPS, USA RTP, USA FedNow

Some instant payment systems use an RTGS-like model where each instant payment is settled individually and immediately in central bank money. SEPA TIPS (TARGET Instant Payment Settlement, operated by ECB) settles each SCT Inst transaction gross in central bank money in real time. Similarly, USA RTP (The Clearing House) and USA FedNow (Federal Reserve) provide immediate finality for each payment. Participants pre-fund a dedicated account and each payment is settled against this prefunded balance — providing true instant settlement finality.

3. Distributed Clearing Approach — Australia NPP

Australia's New Payments Platform (NPP), launched 2018, uses a unique architecture: a Basic Infrastructure (BI) that enables real-time address resolution and messaging, and a Fast Settlement Service (FSS) operated by the Reserve Bank of Australia for real-time gross settlement. Unlike hub models, NPP uses a distributed architecture where participating financial institutions connect bilaterally through the BI infrastructure. Payments are addressed using PayID (a proxy service). Settlement occurs individually and immediately in RBA Exchange Settlement Accounts (ESAs) — making NPP a true RTGS-based instant payment system.

IMPS — Immediate Payment Service (India)

IMPS (Immediate Payment Service), operated by NPCI (National Payments Corporation of India), was launched in 2010 as India's first 24/7 real-time interbank payment system. It predates UPI and remains widely used for direct account-to-account transfers.

IMPS Architecture

NPCI acts as the central hub connecting all member banks. The architecture is a classic hub-and-spoke model:

🏛️

NPCI as Central Hub

NPCI operates the central switch, maintains the MMID directory, routes transactions between member banks, and calculates net settlement positions every 30 minutes.

📲

MMID — Mobile Money Identifier

A unique 7-digit number issued by each bank to a customer, linked to both the customer's mobile number and specific bank account. A customer can have multiple MMIDs — one per bank account registered for IMPS.

🔢

IFSC Code

The Indian Financial System Code (11 characters) identifies the specific bank branch. Used alongside the account number for direct account-to-account IMPS transfers (alternative to MMID-based transfers).

⏱️

Settlement Cycle

NPCI calculates net multilateral positions across all member banks every 30 minutes. RBI then settles the net positions via NEFT settlement cycles, typically 8 times per day.

IMPS Transaction Flow

1

Sender Initiates Transfer

The sender enters the recipient's mobile number + MMID (or account number + IFSC) and the payment amount in their bank's mobile/internet banking app.

2

NPCI Directory Lookup

The sender's bank sends a lookup request to NPCI's central directory. NPCI resolves the mobile number + MMID combination to identify the destination bank and account.

3

NPCI Routes to Destination Bank

NPCI routes the payment instruction to the destination bank in real time. The routing is based on the MMID-to-bank mapping held in NPCI's directory.

4

Destination Bank Credits Account

The destination bank validates the MMID and account, then credits the beneficiary's account in real time. The beneficiary receives an SMS/notification immediately.

5

Confirmation Sent

Both the sending and receiving banks receive confirmation from NPCI. The sender receives a transaction reference number. Net positions are accumulated and settled in the next NPCI settlement cycle (every 30 minutes).

UK Faster Payments — Detailed Architecture

UK Faster Payments Service (FPS) was launched in 2008 and is one of the world's most mature instant payment systems, processing over 4 billion transactions per year. It operates as a 24/7/365 service with a transaction limit of £1 million.

Governance and Infrastructure

📋

Pay.UK — Scheme Manager

Pay.UK owns and manages the Faster Payments scheme rules, participant obligations, and scheme development. It defines the standards, access criteria, and operating rules that all participants must follow.

⚙️

Vocalink — Infrastructure Operator

Vocalink (now part of Mastercard) operates the central technical infrastructure — the central hub that all PSPs connect to for routing and clearing. Vocalink also operates the Image Clearing System (cheque clearing) and BACS.

🏦

Bank of England — Settlement

Settlement occurs across accounts at the Bank of England via CHAPS. Net positions are settled 6 times per day (approximately every 4 hours). Directly connected PSPs must hold settlement accounts at the Bank of England.

🔗

Central Hub Architecture

All PSPs connect to a single central infrastructure. This reduces connectivity requirements — each PSP connects to one hub, not bilaterally to every other PSP. The hub validates, routes, and confirms each payment in real time.

UPI — Unified Payments Interface (India)

UPI (Unified Payments Interface), launched by NPCI in 2016, is India's flagship instant payment system and the world's largest real-time payment system by transaction volume. UPI processes over 10 billion transactions per month and has transformed the payments landscape in India.

UPI Architecture

🏛️

NPCI as Switch

NPCI operates the central UPI switch, which routes payment instructions between banks, resolves VPAs, and maintains the central directory of all registered VPAs and linked accounts. NPCI also manages the UPI ecosystem rules and APIs.

📧

VPA — Virtual Payment Address

A Virtual Payment Address (e.g., rahul@hdfc, 9876543210@okaxis) acts as a proxy for the underlying bank account. The VPA is registered with a bank and mapped to an account. Payers only need the VPA — account number and IFSC are never exposed.

🔐

Two-Factor Authentication

UPI uses two factors: (1) Device binding — the UPI app is registered to a specific mobile device; an OTP is sent to the registered mobile number during setup; (2) UPI PIN — a 4 or 6-digit PIN set by the user for each transaction. Both factors must be present for a payment to succeed.

🏦

PSP Banks and Third-Party Apps

Third-party apps like PhonePe, Google Pay, and Paytm are UPI apps built on top of PSP bank APIs. Each app is sponsored by a PSP bank. The PSP bank handles the banking layer; the app handles the user experience. NPCI certifies all UPI apps.

UPI Payment Features

(a) Push Payments — Standard P2P: The most common use case. The payer initiates a payment by entering the recipient's VPA, amount, and UPI PIN. Funds are debited from the payer's account and credited to the recipient's account in seconds. Used for splitting bills, peer transfers, paying friends.
(b) Collect / Pull Requests: A payee (recipient) sends a collect request to a payer's VPA. The payer receives a notification and must explicitly approve the request by entering their UPI PIN. This is a "pull" model — money is pulled from the payer only with their active consent. Used for requesting payment from customers, splitting expenses where one person requests from others.
(c) UPI Mandates / AutoPay — Recurring Payments: UPI AutoPay enables standing instructions for recurring payments. The user sets up a mandate specifying: amount (fixed or variable up to a limit), frequency (daily/weekly/monthly/annual), merchant, and validity period. Once set up, recurring debits are executed automatically on schedule — no UPI PIN required for amounts up to ₹15,000 per mandate execution (above ₹15,000 requires one-time PIN approval). Use cases include SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) investments, OTT subscriptions (Netflix, Hotstar), utility bills, EMI repayments, and insurance premiums. Mandates are reversible — the user can pause or cancel at any time from their UPI app.
(d) UPI Lite — Offline Payments: Launched in 2022, UPI Lite is an on-device wallet feature that enables payments of up to ₹500 per transaction without an internet connection and without requiring a UPI PIN. The user loads funds (up to ₹2,000) into the on-device wallet from their linked bank account. Small payments are processed locally on the device. This dramatically reduces transaction time and eliminates server round-trips for small-value payments. UPI Lite works even in low/no connectivity scenarios — ideal for transit payments, small retail purchases, and areas with poor network coverage.
(e) UPI 123PAY — Feature Phone Payments: Launched in 2022 to include the 400+ million feature phone users in India who cannot use smartphone apps. UPI 123PAY works via: IVR (Interactive Voice Response) — caller dials a number and navigates prompts to make a payment; Missed call based — missed call to a merchant number triggers a callback to confirm payment; Sound-based proximity payments — uses sound waves to initiate payment at point of sale; OEM-enabled apps — pre-installed apps on certain feature phone models. All modes use UPI's underlying infrastructure but without requiring internet data connectivity.

UPI Transaction Flow — End to End

1

Payer Enters Recipient VPA

The payer opens their UPI app (e.g., PhonePe) and enters the recipient's VPA (e.g., merchant@paytm) and payment amount.

2

VPA Validation Request to NPCI

The payer's app sends a VPA validation request to NPCI's switch. NPCI looks up the VPA in its central directory and returns the mapped bank name and account holder name for the payer to confirm.

3

Payer Confirms and Enters UPI PIN

The payer confirms the recipient name (anti-fraud check) and enters their UPI PIN. The PIN is encrypted on the device and never transmitted in plain text.

4

Payer's PSP Bank Validates and Submits to NPCI

The payer's PSP bank authenticates the device binding and validates the encrypted UPI PIN against the registered PIN (stored at the bank). The bank then submits the payment instruction to NPCI's UPI switch.

5

NPCI Routes to Beneficiary Bank

NPCI's switch routes the payment instruction to the beneficiary's bank (identified from the VPA directory lookup). NPCI acts as the central switch — neither bank communicates directly with the other.

6

Beneficiary Bank Credits Account

The beneficiary bank validates the request and credits the beneficiary's bank account mapped to their VPA. The credit is immediate and real-time.

7

Confirmation Returned to NPCI

The beneficiary bank sends a success confirmation back through NPCI. NPCI updates the transaction status and records the net interbank obligation.

8

Both Parties Notified; Interbank Settlement Queued

Both payer and beneficiary receive real-time notifications (SMS and in-app). NPCI accumulates net positions between banks. The Reserve Bank of India settles net positions periodically (aligned to NEFT settlement cycles). Transaction reference (UPI Ref) is provided for records.

NPCI's Technical Role: NPCI operates as both the switch (routing all payments) and the directory (resolving VPAs to accounts). Without NPCI's central directory, no bank could independently resolve another bank's VPA. This is the critical infrastructure that makes UPI's interoperability possible — any UPI app can pay any VPA regardless of which bank the recipient uses.

4.5 Hybrid Retail Payment Systems (Generic)

A hybrid retail payment system combines elements of instant (real-time) clearing with deferred net settlement. The process flow typically works as follows:

1

Real-Time Clearing

The payment instruction is validated, routed, and cleared in real time (seconds). The beneficiary bank immediately credits the customer's account, knowing it will receive funds later.

2

Interbank Obligation Created

The originating bank now owes the beneficiary bank. This obligation is recorded and accumulates with other payment obligations throughout the day.

3

Settlement Cycle

At defined intervals (e.g., every 30 minutes for UK Faster Payments, several times daily for IMPS), net positions across all participants are calculated and settled in central bank money.

4

Finality

Once net settlement occurs, all individual payments in that cycle achieve final settlement. The beneficiary's credit was provisional until this point but in practice is never reversed.

Key Distinction: In hybrid systems, the customer experience is real-time (instant credit) but the interbank settlement is periodic. True RTGS-based instant systems (TIPS, FedNow) provide immediate interbank settlement finality for every individual payment.
