8.1 Overview of Regulatory Structure in the USA

The United States operates one of the most complex and fragmented financial regulatory frameworks in the world. Unlike most countries that use a single financial regulator, the US employs a dual banking system and a multi-agency oversight model with overlapping jurisdictions at both the federal and state levels. The Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act of 2010, enacted in response to the 2008 financial crisis, substantially reorganised US financial oversight — creating the CFPB, establishing FSOC, and designating systemically important financial market utilities (SIFMUs) for enhanced supervision.

Dodd-Frank Act — Payment System Implications

  • Title VIII — Payment, Clearing, and Settlement Supervision: Established enhanced risk management standards for SIFMUs including financial resources, liquidity, governance, and recovery/resolution planning requirements.
  • Durbin Amendment (Section 1075): Directed the Federal Reserve to limit debit card interchange fees. Resulting Regulation II caps interchange at $0.21 + 0.05% per transaction for large issuers (over $10B assets).
  • CFPB Creation: Gave consumers comprehensive protection for payment products including a right to receive remittance disclosures, error resolution rights, and limits on prepaid card fees.
  • Volcker Rule: Restricts proprietary trading by banks, reducing market-making capacity and affecting liquidity in securities markets that settlement systems rely upon.
Dual Banking System: US banks may be chartered federally (national banks — OCC) or at the state level (state banks — state regulators). State banks that join the Federal Reserve System are also supervised by the Fed. This creates potentially three overlapping layers of supervision. The result is a more complex compliance environment than single-regulator jurisdictions like the UK (PRA/FCA) or EU (ECB/national competent authorities).

8.2 USA Payments Landscape

The United States operates a rich and diverse payments ecosystem combining Federal Reserve-operated public infrastructure with private sector networks. The US lacks a single unified retail payment infrastructure — instead, multiple complementary systems serve different payment needs, from large-value interbank transfers to instant retail payments and consumer P2P.

🏛️

Fedwire Funds Service

Federal Reserve RTGS for large-value, time-critical USD payments. ~$4 trillion settled daily across ~650,000 transactions. Operated by the Fed, accessible to ~9,000 financial institutions.

🏦

CHIPS

Clearing House Interbank Payments System — private sector large-value system by The Clearing House. ~50 participant banks, ~$1.8 trillion daily. Multilateral netting with Fedwire final settlement.

🔄

ACH Network

Automated Clearing House — batch EFT network with two operators: FedACH and EPN (TCH). ~30 billion transactions per year covering payroll, bill pay, vendor payments, and consumer debits.

⚡

RTP Network

Real-Time Payments by The Clearing House. Launched 2017, 24/7/365, up to $1M per transaction. Covers ~90% of US demand deposit accounts via participating banks.

⚡

FedNow

Federal Reserve's own instant payment service, launched July 2023. 24/7/365 real-time gross settlement, $500,000 transaction limit. Directly competes with RTP, open to all Fed-eligible institutions.

📱

Zelle

P2P network operated by Early Warning Services (owned by major US banks). Integrated into banking apps — processed over $800B in 2023. Uses RTP or ACH rails depending on sending/receiving bank capabilities.

💳

Card Networks

Visa, Mastercard (four-party open loop), American Express and Discover (three-party closed loop). Process billions of debit/credit card transactions. PIN debit uses PIN debit networks (STAR, NYCE, Pulse).

📋

Check 21 / Image Clearing

Check Clearing for the 21st Century Act (2004) enabled electronic check image exchange. ~6 billion checks annually remain, declining rapidly. Federal Reserve Check Services clears check images.

8.3 Overview of Fedwire Funds Service

The Fedwire Funds Service is the United States' primary large-value Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) system, operated by the twelve Federal Reserve Banks. Each payment settles individually, immediately, and irrevocably at the moment it is processed — there is no netting, no end-of-day batch, and no reversal once settlement occurs. This makes Fedwire the backbone of the US financial system for time-critical, large-value dollar payments.

⚡

Real-Time Gross Settlement

Every Fedwire payment settles individually and instantly. No batching or netting. Settlement is final and irrevocable at the moment of processing. Eliminates intraday credit risk between participants.

🏛️

Federal Reserve Operated

Operated by the 12 Federal Reserve Banks collectively. The Federal Reserve Bank of New York plays a central processing role given New York's dominance in US wholesale financial markets.

💰

Scale — ~$4 Trillion Daily

Processes approximately $4 trillion in value daily — roughly equivalent to the entire US annual GDP processed every single business day. Critical for interbank settlement, fed funds market, and correspondent banking.

🏦

Broad Participation

~9,000 financial institutions hold Federal Reserve accounts and can use Fedwire. Includes commercial banks, savings institutions, credit unions, and US branches of foreign banks.

Account Types on Fedwire

Financial institutions that participate in Fedwire hold accounts at their local Federal Reserve Bank. These are called master accounts (formally Reserve Accounts or Fed accounts). Balances in these accounts represent claims on the Federal Reserve — they are the highest form of settlement asset in USD, as they carry no credit risk (the Fed cannot default on USD obligations).

  • Master Account: Primary account held at a Reserve Bank. Used for all Fed services including Fedwire, ACH, and minimum reserve requirements. Earns interest on excess reserves (IOER/IORB).
  • Joint Account: Shared account arrangement where multiple institutions pool liquidity for settlement purposes.
  • Pass-Through Reserve Account: Account maintained by a correspondent bank on behalf of smaller respondent banks for reserve and settlement purposes.

Settlement Finality

Under Regulation J (12 CFR Part 210), a Fedwire funds transfer is final at the moment the Federal Reserve Bank credits the receiving institution's account. This means:

  • Payment cannot be recalled or reversed after settlement occurs
  • Receiving bank can release funds to its customer immediately upon receipt
  • No counterparty risk for the receiving bank — the Fed's credit is risk-free
  • Provides legal certainty under the UCC (Uniform Commercial Code) Article 4A framework for funds transfers

Fedwire Message Format and ISO 20022 Migration

Fedwire historically uses a proprietary fixed-length tag-value format. Key identifiers include the IMAD (Input Message Accountability Data) and OMAD (Output Message Accountability Data) — unique identifiers that allow tracking of individual payments. The Fed has announced migration to ISO 20022 messaging aligned with the broader industry migration and CHIPS transition.

8.4 Fedwire Securities Service

The Fedwire Securities Service is the Federal Reserve's book-entry system for the safekeeping, transfer, and settlement of US government and federal agency securities. It is a critical component of US financial market infrastructure — providing the settlement backbone for US Treasury securities, which are the world's pre-eminent safe asset and the benchmark for global risk-free rates.

What is Book-Entry Settlement?

Book-entry means securities exist only as electronic records — there are no physical certificates. Ownership and transfer are recorded by simple debit/credit entries on the Fedwire Securities Service ledger. The Federal Reserve Banks act as the fiscal agent for the US Treasury and as custodian for the book-entry securities.

🏛️

US Treasury Securities

T-Bills (short-term, discount), T-Notes (2–10 year), T-Bonds (20–30 year), TIPS (inflation-indexed), FRNs (floating rate notes). All issued and settled via Fedwire Securities. The US Treasury conducts auctions via TreasuryDirect; primary dealers receive allocations settled via Fedwire.

🏦

Agency Securities

Securities issued by US government-sponsored enterprises (GSEs): Fannie Mae (FNMA), Freddie Mac (FHLMC), Federal Home Loan Banks, Farm Credit System. Also Federal Reserve Bank securities. All settled via Fedwire Securities in book-entry form.

🔄

DvP Settlement

Delivery versus Payment (DvP) — securities and funds transfer simultaneously. When a buyer purchases Treasuries, Fedwire Securities moves the securities to the buyer's custody account while simultaneously Fedwire Funds (or an interlinked mechanism) debits the buyer's cash account. Eliminates principal risk.

🔒

Federal Reserve as Custodian

The Fed acts as custodian for foreign central banks and international official institutions holding US Treasury securities as foreign exchange reserves. This is one of the largest custodial relationships in the world — foreign central banks hold over $7 trillion in US Treasuries.

Fedwire Securities Operating Model

Fedwire Securities operates through a two-tier structure:

1

Federal Reserve Level (Tier 1)

Federal Reserve Banks hold securities in book-entry form directly for large institutions (primary dealers, large commercial banks, foreign central banks, government agencies). These holders receive securities credited directly to their Fedwire Securities accounts. Transfers between Tier 1 holders settle in real time via the Fedwire Securities system.

2

Depository Trust Company / Custodian Level (Tier 2)

DTC (part of DTCC) holds a large omnibus account at Fedwire Securities on behalf of its broker-dealer and institutional investor participants. DTC handles intra-DTC securities settlement for the broader market — most retail and institutional Treasury transactions settle within DTC using Fedwire Securities as the ultimate custodian.

3

Real-Time Gross Settlement of Securities

Securities transfers on Fedwire Securities settle on a gross, real-time basis — each transfer settles immediately and irrevocably, just like Fedwire Funds. The linked DvP mechanism ensures securities and cash move simultaneously, eliminating principal risk in Treasury market transactions.

Scale of Fedwire Securities

Fedwire Securities processes approximately $10–12 trillion in securities transfers on peak days (for example, at Treasury auction settlement dates), making it one of the largest securities settlement systems in the world. The total value of book-entry securities on Fedwire Securities exceeds $30 trillion, reflecting the enormous size of the US government securities market.

8.5 Daylight Overdraft / Intraday Credit

The Federal Reserve allows banks to overdraft their Federal Reserve master accounts during the business day — a facility known as daylight overdrafts (also called intraday credit). This is an essential feature of the Fedwire system: without it, banks would need to pre-fund the full $4+ trillion in daily Fedwire value at the start of each day, which would be operationally impossible and would require an enormous amount of high-quality liquid assets to be held idle.

Why Daylight Overdrafts Are Essential: On a typical day, Fedwire processes approximately $4 trillion in payments. If every bank had to pre-fund 100% of its outgoing payments before sending, the system would require over $4 trillion in reserves sitting idle every morning — an impossible capital constraint. Daylight overdrafts allow banks to send payments using incoming funds that have not yet arrived, trusting that the intraday flow of receipts will cover the position by end of day.

Net Debit Cap — Maximum Intraday Overdraft

Each bank's maximum intraday overdraft is called its Net Debit Cap. The cap is a capital-based measure calculated as a multiplier applied to the bank's capital measure (a combination of adjusted capital and risk-weighted assets). There are four tiers of net debit cap:

Cap CategoryMultiplierEligibilityNotes
ExemptZero — no overdraft allowedInstitutions that never overdraft or choose not toInstitution voluntarily restricts itself; no fee exposure
De MinimisSmall dollar cap (~$10M)Institutions with minimal daylight overdraft needsStreamlined approval process; no formal self-assessment required
Self-AssessedUp to 20% of capital measureInstitutions that complete Federal Reserve self-assessmentMost common tier; requires board-approved funds management policy and self-assessment of creditworthiness, intraday liquidity management, and operational controls
Board-Approved (Maximum)Up to 40% of capital measureInstitutions with Fed Board approval based on examinationHighest cap; requires Federal Reserve Board approval and demonstrates strongest operational and financial standing

Daylight Overdraft Fees

The Federal Reserve charges a fee on daylight overdraft usage to create incentives for prudent intraday liquidity management. The fee is calculated on the average daylight overdraft position throughout the day:

  • Fee rate: 50 basis points (0.50%) per annum, applied to average intraday overdraft usage
  • Daily equivalent: The annualised rate divided by 360 days, applied to each day's average overdraft position
  • Example: A bank with an average intraday overdraft of $1 billion would pay approximately $13,889 per day (0.50% × $1B ÷ 360)
  • Minimum charge: $25 per day if any overdraft usage occurs

Collateral-Based (Free) Daylight Overdrafts

Banks can eliminate daylight overdraft fees entirely by pledging eligible collateral to the Federal Reserve:

  • Banks pledge securities (typically US Treasuries, agency securities, or other eligible collateral) to the Federal Reserve under a collateral pledge agreement
  • The pledged collateral creates a collateral-based overdraft capacity equal to the market value of collateral pledged (subject to haircuts)
  • Intraday overdrafts within the collateral value are fee-free — the Federal Reserve treats collateralised intraday credit as essentially risk-free to the Fed
  • Banks with large intraday liquidity needs typically pledge collateral rather than incur fees; collateral pledging is administratively simpler for consistent large users
  • Collateral is returned at end of day once the overdraft is covered by end-of-day flows
Collateral vs. Fee-Based: A bank choosing between fee-based and collateral-based overdrafts makes an economic decision: is the cost of tying up collateral (opportunity cost) higher or lower than the 50bp fee? For banks with large securities portfolios and significant intraday funding needs, collateral pledging is almost always cheaper. For banks with occasional small overdrafts, paying the fee is simpler administratively.

Peak Overdraft Timing and Statistics

Daylight overdrafts follow a predictable intraday pattern driven by payment timing conventions in the US financial system:

Average daylight overdraft across the full day is considerably lower, approximately $50–80 billion system-wide. The majority of daylight overdraft usage is collateral-based and therefore fee-free — estimated 80%+ of aggregate overdraft usage is covered by pledged collateral.

8.7 Fedwire ISO 20022 Migration

The Federal Reserve announced a comprehensive migration of the Fedwire Funds Service to ISO 20022 messaging standards, coordinated with The Clearing House's parallel migration of CHIPS. This is one of the most significant infrastructure changes in US payment history — modernising the messaging backbone of the largest value payment systems in the world's reserve currency.

Migration Scope and Message Types

Fedwire's ISO 20022 migration is based on the payment message standard (pacs — Payments Clearing and Settlement) family, with Fedwire-specific extensions to accommodate US market requirements:

ISO 20022 MessageFull NameReplacesUse on Fedwire
pacs.008FIToFICustomerCreditTransferCTR / CTP (Business Function Codes)Customer credit transfers — Bank A sends customer payment to Bank B. Carries full beneficiary, debtor, and structured remittance data in ISO 20022 format.
pacs.009FinancialInstitutionCreditTransferBTR / BTP (Business Function Codes)Interbank/treasury transfers between financial institutions. Used for correspondent banking, bank-to-bank liquidity movements, Fed funds transactions.
pacs.028FIToFIPaymentStatusRequestSVC (status inquiry)Payment status requests — replaces the proprietary SVC service message for status inquiries and payment tracing on Fedwire.

Fedwire-Specific ISO 20022 Extensions

The Federal Reserve has published Fedwire-specific extensions to the base ISO 20022 message specifications. These extensions accommodate features that are unique to Fedwire and have no direct ISO 20022 standard equivalent:

  • IMAD/OMAD fields: Fedwire's Input and Output Message Accountability Data identifiers are retained as Fedwire-specific supplementary data fields within the ISO 20022 message envelope — they are too important for audit and reconciliation to eliminate
  • Fedwire Business Function Code equivalents: ISO 20022 purpose codes map to Fedwire business function codes; the Fed has published a mapping table
  • Fedwire-specific validation rules: ABA routing numbers, Federal Reserve processing conventions, and US-specific data fields are accommodated in the Fedwire ISO 20022 implementation guide

Coordinated Migration with CHIPS

The Federal Reserve and The Clearing House are migrating Fedwire and CHIPS to ISO 20022 on a coordinated timeline. This coordination is essential because many large-value USD payments flow through both systems on the same day — a payment may enter via CHIPS (netting) and be finally settled via Fedwire. If both systems are not on ISO 20022 simultaneously, banks would need to translate messages at each system handoff point, adding complexity and cost. The coordinated approach means:

  • Banks need to update their payment systems once for both Fedwire and CHIPS ISO 20022
  • CHIPS UID-to-BIC/LEI mapping and Fedwire ABA-to-BIC mapping are being standardised together
  • Industry testing and parallel run periods are aligned across both operators

Impact on Banks and Financial Institutions

The Fedwire ISO 20022 migration requires significant system changes across every participant bank:

💻

Payment System Updates

Core banking and payment processing systems must be updated to generate pacs.008/pacs.009 format messages outbound and parse ISO 20022 inbound messages. This affects payment engines, SWIFT interfaces, and Fedwire connectivity layers.

🔍

AML/Sanctions Screening

AML and sanctions screening systems must be updated to handle ISO 20022's richer, structured data fields. ISO 20022 carries structured name/address data (vs. free-text in proprietary format) which improves screening accuracy but requires updated screening logic and field mapping.

📊

Reconciliation Systems

Back-office reconciliation must be updated to match IMAD/OMAD references within ISO 20022 envelopes, map ISO 20022 purpose codes to internal payment categories, and handle the richer remittance data that ISO 20022 carries.

📋

Regulatory Reporting

Regulatory reporting systems (including BSA/AML reports, large value transaction reports, and FRBNY statistical reports) must be updated to extract data from ISO 20022 message fields rather than proprietary Fedwire tag fields.

SWIFT Coexistence Note: Unlike the SWIFT CBPR+ (Cross-Border Payments and Reporting Plus) ISO 20022 migration where a coexistence period allows MT and MX messages simultaneously, Fedwire ISO 20022 migration is a domestic US system. Fedwire is not SWIFT-based for domestic flows — domestic Fedwire participants connect directly via FedLine (the Federal Reserve's connectivity platform). There is no SWIFT MT-to-MX coexistence issue for Fedwire itself. The SWIFT coexistence complexity applies only to the international SWIFT messaging layer that feeds instructions into US correspondent banks, which then submit to Fedwire.

8.8 UETR-to-IMAD Linkage

One of the most technically complex challenges in modern cross-border USD payments is maintaining end-to-end payment tracking when a GPI payment (identified by a UETR — Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference) passes through Fedwire (identified by IMAD/OMAD). These are two entirely different reference number systems, and bridging them is essential for SWIFT GPI's payment tracking promise to function for USD payments settled via Fedwire.

The Two Reference Systems

Fedwire IMAD / OMAD

Fedwire uses Input Message Accountability Data (IMAD) and Output Message Accountability Data (OMAD) as its primary payment references. These are assigned by the Federal Reserve at the time of processing.

IMAD Format: YYYYMMDDBBBBBBBBSSSSSSSS Where: YYYYMMDD = Processing date (8 digits) BBBBBBBB = Sending bank ABA routing number (9 digits, but 8 used in IMAD) SSSSSSSS = Sequence number assigned by Fed (8 digits, zero-padded) Example IMAD: 20240315021000021000012345 OMAD Format: Similar structure, assigned by Fed to the receiving side of the same payment for the receiving bank's records.

SWIFT GPI UETR

SWIFT GPI uses a UETR (Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference) — a UUID (Universally Unique Identifier, Version 4) that is a 128-bit random number formatted as a 36-character string. The UETR is assigned at the point of payment initiation by the first GPI member bank and must be preserved unchanged through every correspondent bank and settlement system in the payment chain.

UETR Format (UUID v4): xxxxxxxx-xxxx-4xxx-yxxx-xxxxxxxxxxxx Where x = random hexadecimal digit 4 = fixed version indicator y = 8, 9, a, or b (variant indicator) Example UETR: e2a7e0df-3b91-4b5c-a4d8-2c1f9b3e5a7d Used in: - SWIFT MT 103 field :121: - ISO 20022 pacs.008 UETR element - SWIFT GPI Tracker database key

The Linkage Challenge

When a cross-border payment arrives at a US correspondent bank via SWIFT (carrying a UETR), and the correspondent bank then submits that payment to Fedwire (which assigns an IMAD), the two reference systems must be linked. The SWIFT GPI Tracker needs to know that UETR "e2a7e0df-..." corresponds to IMAD "20240315021000021000012345" so that the GPI tracker can be updated to show that Fedwire settlement has occurred.

Technical Solution — UETR Embedding and Internal Mapping

1

Extract UETR from Incoming SWIFT MT 103

The US correspondent bank's payment processing system receives the inbound SWIFT MT 103 (or pacs.008 in ISO 20022). The UETR is extracted from field :121: (in MT 103) or the UETR element in pacs.008. The UETR is stored in the correspondent bank's internal payment record along with all other payment details.

2

Include UETR in Fedwire Payment Reference

When the US correspondent bank submits the Fedwire payment instruction, the UETR is included in the Fedwire message. In the current proprietary format, the UETR can be embedded in Fedwire's Tag {9}: (Fedwire's remittance/reference data field), or in the payment details/reference fields available in the Fedwire message. In the ISO 20022 Fedwire format, the UETR maps to the end-to-end identification element in pacs.008.

3

Fedwire Processes and Assigns IMAD/OMAD

The Federal Reserve processes the Fedwire payment and assigns a unique IMAD (sent to the originating bank's Fedwire acknowledgement) and OMAD (delivered to the receiving bank). These are stored in the bank's Fedwire transaction records. At this moment, the UETR and the IMAD/OMAD co-exist in the correspondent bank's internal systems but are separate references.

4

Build UETR ↔ IMAD/OMAD Mapping Table

The US correspondent bank's payment operations system creates an internal mapping record linking the UETR to the IMAD and OMAD. This mapping table is the critical bridge between the GPI world (UETR) and the Fedwire world (IMAD/OMAD). Compliance, reconciliation, and investigations teams use this table when responding to payment status queries — a customer asking "where is my wire?" triggers a lookup: UETR → IMAD → Fedwire status.

5

Update SWIFT GPI Tracker with Fedwire Confirmation

The US correspondent bank updates the SWIFT GPI Tracker using the UETR as the key, confirming: (a) the payment has been settled via Fedwire; (b) the settlement date and time; (c) the IMAD and/or OMAD as supplementary reference data (so the beneficiary bank can trace through Fedwire if needed); (d) any fee deducted; (e) the status code (ACCC — "Accepted Credit to Customer Account" or equivalent GPI status). This closes the loop on the GPI tracking chain.

Practical Impact: Large US correspondent banks (JPMorgan, Citibank, Bank of America, Wells Fargo) handle hundreds of thousands of these UETR-IMAD linkages daily. They maintain sophisticated internal payment reference databases that hold the UETR-to-IMAD mapping alongside FX rates applied, fees charged, processing timestamps, and compliance screening outcomes. When a foreign bank or corporate client queries GPI Tracker for payment status, the entire chain of events — from SWIFT receipt to Fedwire settlement — is visible because of this linkage.

8.9 Federal Funds Market and Fedwire

The Federal Funds Market is the overnight lending market for US dollar reserve balances held at the Federal Reserve. When a bank has excess reserves at the Fed (more than required/desired for operational needs), it can lend those reserves overnight to another bank that has a shortfall. These transactions are executed and settled via Fedwire — making Fedwire the essential settlement rail for the Fed Funds market and a critical channel through which monetary policy is transmitted to the US financial system.

How the Federal Funds Market Works

1

Banks Identify Reserve Positions

Late in the business day (typically after 3:00 PM ET), banks calculate their end-of-day reserve position. Banks with surplus reserves (excess above their target operating balance) look to lend. Banks with shortfalls (below their required/desired balance) look to borrow. Fed Funds brokers (e.g., Tradition, Garban) facilitate matching between lenders and borrowers.

2

Loan Agreement at the Fed Funds Rate

Lender and borrower agree on an amount and rate. The rate agreed is the Fed Funds Rate (FFR) — the rate targeted by FOMC policy. Transactions are typically overnight (term until next business day) though term Fed Funds (multi-day) also exist. Loans are unsecured — there is no collateral; lending banks rely on the creditworthiness of the borrowing bank.

3

Fedwire Settles the Reserve Transfer

To execute the Fed Funds loan, the lending bank sends a Fedwire BTR (Bank Transfer) to the borrowing bank — debiting its own Fed master account and crediting the borrower's Fed master account. The Fedwire transfer is the actual movement of reserve balances. This is why Fedwire's late operating hours (until 6:30 PM ET) are critical — they accommodate end-of-day Fed Funds settlement activity.

4

Repayment the Following Morning

The next business day, the borrowing bank sends a Fedwire BTR back to the lender for the principal plus one day's interest at the agreed Fed Funds Rate. This repayment is one of the first transactions each morning, contributing to early-day Fedwire volume.

Fed Funds Market Statistics and Fedwire Volume

The Federal Funds market, while large in notional terms, has declined significantly in volume since the Federal Reserve began paying IOER/IORB in 2008 (which reduced the incentive to lend excess reserves at market rates):

  • Daily Fed Funds volume: Approximately $80–120 billion per day in Fed Funds transactions via Fedwire (including overnight Fed Funds and term Fed Funds)
  • Primary participants: Large commercial banks (net borrowers or lenders depending on intraday flows), Federal Home Loan Banks (historically large lenders of excess reserves into Fed Funds market), money center banks, foreign bank branches
  • EFFR (Effective Fed Funds Rate): The volume-weighted median of overnight Fed Funds transactions, published daily by the New York Fed — this is the benchmark rate that monetary policy targets
Fedwire's Role in Monetary Policy Transmission: By providing the settlement rails for Fed Funds transactions, Fedwire is a direct mechanism through which the Federal Reserve's monetary policy decisions are transmitted to the financial system. When the Fed adjusts its FOMC target range or IORB rate, the effects ripple through the Fed Funds market — and the trades that reflect those effects settle via Fedwire BTR transactions every business day.

8.10 Fedwire Support to SWIFT GPI

SWIFT GPI (Global Payments Innovation) has transformed cross-border correspondent banking by providing end-to-end payment tracking, speed commitments, and transparency on fees and FX rates. For USD cross-border payments, Fedwire plays an essential role as the domestic USD settlement backbone that ultimately settles the US-side leg of international dollar transactions.

SWIFT GPI Overview

SWIFT GPI introduced several key elements for cross-border payments:

  • UETR (Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference): A UUID (128-bit unique identifier) assigned to each GPI payment at origination and carried through all correspondent banks in the payment chain. Enables end-to-end tracking via the SWIFT Tracker.
  • gpi Tracker: Cloud-based database where each correspondent bank in the chain confirms processing and forwards the UETR. Originating banks and their customers can see real-time status of where the payment is in the correspondent chain.
  • Speed Commitments: GPI member banks commit to same-day use of funds for payments received before cutoff times. For USD payments, this typically means completion within hours rather than days.
  • Fee Transparency: Each correspondent bank must report what fee they deducted, so the beneficiary receives the net amount and the originator knows the deduction chain.

Role of Fedwire in USD Cross-Border Payments

For cross-border USD payments using correspondent banking, the payment flow typically involves:

1

Non-US Bank Initiates via Correspondent

A non-US bank (e.g., a German bank) sends a SWIFT MT 103 (or pacs.008 in ISO 20022) payment to its US correspondent bank (e.g., JPMorgan or Citibank). The message carries the UETR and payment details. The German bank's nostro account at the US correspondent will be debited.

2

US Correspondent Processes via Fedwire or CHIPS

The US correspondent bank receives the SWIFT instruction and executes a USD payment to the beneficiary's bank. For large-value or time-critical payments, this is done via Fedwire Funds (RTGS). For net-settled interbank payments, it may route via CHIPS. The SWIFT UETR is carried in the payment reference data.

3

Fedwire Settles Final USD Leg

Fedwire processes the payment RTGS — debiting the sending bank's Fed master account and crediting the receiving bank's Fed master account. Settlement is final and irrevocable. The UETR can be referenced via the Fedwire payment reference fields.

4

GPI Tracker Updated

Each bank in the chain updates the SWIFT GPI Tracker with payment status. Originating bank and beneficiary bank can see the full chain of events, timing, fees, and FX rates applied. The UETR links the SWIFT GPI record to the underlying Fedwire settlement.
