7.1 STEP2 — Pan-European ACH Overview

What is STEP2?

STEP2 is the Pan-European Automated Clearing House (PE-ACH) operated by EBA CLEARING. It was launched in 2003 and is today the primary multilateral clearing infrastructure for SEPA payment schemes across 36 SEPA countries. STEP2 processes four schemes:

📤

SCT — SEPA Credit Transfer

Push credit transfer: payer's PSP sends funds to payee's PSP. Same-day or next-business-day settlement. Batch-cycle processing with multiple clearing windows per business day.

📥

SDD Core — Direct Debit Core

Pull collection for consumer debtors. Creditor initiates collection under a signed mandate. Debtors retain unconditional refund rights within defined windows.

🏢

SDD B2B — Direct Debit Business

Pull collection for business debtors only. Stricter mandate validation at debtor bank. No unconditional refund right. Tighter exception timeframes.

⚡

SCT Inst — Instant Credit Transfer

Real-time push credit transfer. 10-second end-to-end maximum. 24/7/365 availability. €100,000 per-transaction scheme limit. Processed via RT1 (continuous) or TIPS.

Network Topology and Scale

STEP2 operates a hub-and-spoke model connecting over 2,000 Payment Service Providers (PSPs) across the SEPA zone through a single centralised platform. PSPs connect directly to STEP2 as Direct Participants or access the system indirectly through a Direct Participant acting as their gateway. This architecture means any PSP in the network can exchange payments with any other PSP through a single multilateral infrastructure — removing the need for thousands of bilateral agreements.

PSPs connected to STEP2 include commercial banks, savings banks, credit unions, electronic money institutions, and payment institutions licensed under PSD2. National central banks participate indirectly via their settlement connection to T2.

CGS — Clearing and Settlement Model

STEP2 operates under the CGS (Clearing and Settlement) model in which STEP2 performs clearing — validating, disaggregating, sorting, and calculating net positions — while T2 performs settlement of those net positions in central bank money. The settlement leg works as follows:

CGS Settlement Flow: After each clearing cycle, STEP2 calculates the multilateral net position of every participating PSP. STEP2 submits a settlement file (via pacs.010 Ancillary System Settlement Instruction) to T2. T2 debits or credits each PSP's MCA (Main Cash Account) or designated Technical Account by the net settlement amount. T2 confirms settlement completion back to STEP2, which then releases output files to receiving PSPs.

This model means SEPA payments ultimately settle in risk-free central bank money — the highest quality settlement asset — despite being cleared through a private infrastructure.

7.2 SCT Processing and Exceptions

SCT Processing Lifecycle — 7 Steps

The SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) is a push payment initiated by the payer's PSP. The end-to-end lifecycle from instruction submission to final crediting of the beneficiary follows seven steps:

1

Customer Initiation → PSP A (pain.001)

The originating customer submits a payment instruction to PSP A (their bank) via a pain.001 Customer Credit Transfer Initiation message — either as a single transaction or as a batch file. PSP A validates: IBAN format, sanctions screening, AML checks, and available balance.

2

PSP A Submits to STEP2 (pacs.008 batch)

PSP A submits a batch file to STEP2 containing individual pacs.008 FI to FI Customer Credit Transfer messages. The batch must reach STEP2 within the relevant submission window for the target clearing cycle. STEP2 acknowledges receipt and begins validation.

3

STEP2 Validates and Disaggregates

STEP2 validates each transaction in the batch: format compliance with ISO 20022 SCT schema, BIC/IBAN consistency, scheme rule compliance (amount in euros, SEPA country codes, mandatory fields). Invalid transactions are rejected back to PSP A with reason codes. Valid transactions are disaggregated from the batch and routed individually.

4

STEP2 Routes pacs.008 to PSP B

STEP2 routes each valid pacs.008 instruction to the receiving PSP B based on the beneficiary's BIC (derived from the IBAN via the SEPA BIC directory). Net positions between all PSP pairs are accumulated across the clearing cycle.

5

STEP2 Submits Net Settlement File to T2 (pacs.010)

At end of each clearing cycle, STEP2 calculates the multilateral net position for every PSP in the cycle and submits a settlement instruction (pacs.010 Ancillary System Settlement Instruction) to T2's ancillary system interface (ASI). Each PSP's net debit or net credit for the cycle is consolidated into a single settlement amount.

6

T2 Settles MCAs; Confirms to STEP2

T2 debits the net debit PSPs and credits the net credit PSPs across their Main Cash Accounts (MCAs) or Technical Accounts. Settlement is in central bank money. T2 returns settlement confirmation to STEP2 upon successful completion. If settlement fails (insufficient MCA balance), STEP2 applies its failure-to-settle procedures.

7

STEP2 Delivers to PSP B; PSP B Credits Beneficiary

Following settlement confirmation, STEP2 releases output files to PSP B containing the individual pacs.008 instructions settled in that cycle. PSP B credits the beneficiary's account and may send a camt.054 Debit/Credit Notification to the beneficiary customer.

Recall Process — camt.056 → pacs.004 or camt.029

The recall process is one of the most operationally significant exception flows in SEPA. There are two timeframe tracks:

Same-Day Recall (Reversal — pacs.007)

When a duplicate or erroneous payment is identified on the same business day as settlement, PSP A can send a pacs.007 Payment Reversal directly via STEP2. This is the faster track — it travels through the ACH clearing infrastructure using the same-day cycle and reverses the funds more quickly than a standard recall. The pacs.007 must reference the original pacs.008 End-to-End ID and Transaction ID. PSP B must credit the reversal upon receipt if the beneficiary's account still holds the funds.
Standard Recall (up to 13 months — camt.056)

For payments discovered after the settlement date, PSP A sends a camt.056 FI to FI Payment Cancellation Request to PSP B (routed via STEP2 or bilaterally). The camt.056 must contain a valid recall reason (DUPL, FRAD, or TECH). PSP B has 15 business days to respond. If PSP B agrees, it returns the funds via pacs.004 Payment Return. If PSP B refuses (e.g., beneficiary will not return funds), PSP B sends a camt.029 Resolution of Investigation with a negative response. The standard recall window is 13 months from the original settlement date. After 13 months, PSP A must pursue civil recovery outside the scheme.

SDD Mandate Validation Mechanics — Deep Dive

The mandate validation mechanic is the most fundamental difference between the two SDD schemes and has significant operational implications for both creditor banks (ODFIs) and debtor banks (RDFIs):

SDD Core — Mandate Mechanics

Step 1: Creditor issues a mandate to the debtor. The mandate contains a Unique Mandate Reference (UMR) — a reference unique to the creditor-debtor relationship — and the creditor's Creditor Identifier (CI) — a unique pan-European identifier issued to the creditor by their national authority.

Step 2: The debtor signs the mandate and returns it to the creditor. The creditor stores the mandate.

Step 3: The debtor bank (RDFI) receives the SDD Core collection via STEP2. It does not pre-validate against any stored mandate — there is none. It validates only that the account exists, is active, and has not been blocked by the debtor.

Step 4: If the debtor later disputes the collection (claims the mandate was not authorised or was exceeded), the debtor claims a refund via their bank using an R-transaction. The debtor bank processes the refund using the 8-week or 13-month window and debits the creditor bank's net position in the next clearing cycle.

Key principle: the creditor carries the mandate risk in Core — the system relies on the creditor holding valid mandates and the dispute resolution process to handle disputes post-payment.
SDD B2B — Mandate Mechanics

Step 1: Creditor issues a mandate (containing UMR and CI) and the debtor business signs it.

Step 2: The debtor submits the mandate details to their own bank (RDFI). The RDFI stores the mandate in its mandate register, recording: CI, UMR, debtor account, creditor account, mandate type (one-off or recurring), and any amount/frequency constraints.

Step 3: When the creditor initiates a collection via STEP2, the RDFI receives the pacs.003 instruction. Before processing, the RDFI cross-checks: (a) Is there a registered mandate with this CI and UMR? (b) Does the collection match the mandate parameters?

Step 4: If no matching mandate is registered, or if the collection deviates from mandate parameters, the RDFI must return the collection (MD01 — No Mandate, or MD06 — Mandate Cancelled). The collection never reaches the debtor's account.

Step 5: If the mandate is valid, the RDFI executes the debit. There is no unconditional refund right — the RDFI has pre-validated.

Key principle: the RDFI carries mandate validation responsibility in B2B — this shifts risk from post-payment dispute to pre-payment validation, enabling the no-refund rule.
Creditor Identifier (CI) — What It Is and Why It Matters

The Creditor Identifier (CI) is a unique alphanumeric identifier assigned to every SDD creditor by the relevant national authority (e.g., Bundesbank in Germany, Banque de France in France). The CI has a standardised pan-European format: 2-character country code + 2-digit check digits + 3-character creditor business code + up to 28-character national identifier. Example: DE98ZZZ09999999999. The CI is included in every SDD instruction — both Core and B2B — and allows debtor banks to identify the creditor uniquely across SEPA. In SDD B2B, the CI is the primary key used by the RDFI to look up stored mandates.

7.4 SCT Inst — SEPA Instant Credit Transfer

EPC SCT Inst Scheme Rules

The SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) scheme is governed by the EPC (European Payments Council) SCT Inst Rulebook, updated annually. Core scheme parameters:

⏱

10-Second End-to-End Maximum

From the moment the originating PSP submits the pacs.008 to the CSM, to the moment the beneficiary PSP confirms acceptance, the entire processing chain must complete within 10 seconds. This is not 10 seconds for the CSM alone — it is the total end-to-end envelope including routing, beneficiary PSP validation, and confirmation.

🕐

24/7/365 Availability

SCT Inst operates every day of the year with no scheduled downtime — including weekends, public holidays, and year-end. This is a fundamental departure from batch ACH systems which operate only on business days within defined windows. PSPs offering SCT Inst must maintain continuous operational availability.

💶

€100,000 Per-Transaction Limit

The scheme maximum is €100,000 per individual SCT Inst transaction. This was raised from the original €25,000 limit in 2019 to broaden the range of use cases (including B2B and high-value consumer payments). Individual PSPs may set lower limits for their customers but may not exceed the scheme maximum.

🌍

Mandatory Participation (EU)

From 9 January 2025, EU PSPs that offer SCT must also offer inbound SCT Inst receipt. From 9 October 2025, outbound SCT Inst sending is also mandatory for EU PSPs. Non-EU SEPA country PSPs (e.g., UK, Switzerland) have later deadlines: inbound January 2027, outbound October 2027.

The 25-Second Exception Window

The SCT Inst scheme recognises that under exceptional conditions — network congestion, beneficiary PSP system degradation, complex sanctions screening — a response within 10 seconds may not always be achievable. The scheme provides a precisely defined exception handling mechanism:

How the 25-Second Window Works:

0–10 seconds: Normal processing window. The beneficiary PSP is expected to validate the incoming pacs.008, check the beneficiary account, run fraud/sanctions screening, and return a pacs.002 ACCP (accepted) or RJCT (rejected) to the CSM within this window.

10–25 seconds (exception window): If the beneficiary PSP cannot respond within 10 seconds, it has an additional 15-second window in which it must either (a) send pacs.002 ACCP to confirm acceptance, or (b) send pacs.002 RJCT to decline. This gives a total maximum processing time of 25 seconds from submission.

After 25 seconds: If no response has been received from the beneficiary PSP within 25 seconds, the CSM generates an automatic timeout return — the transaction is treated as failed. The originating PSP's account is not debited, and the originator is notified of the timeout failure via pacs.002 RJCT (or equivalent negative confirmation). The originator customer is refunded immediately.

The 25-second maximum is therefore not a target — it is the absolute outer boundary. PSPs are expected to respond well within 10 seconds under normal conditions. Persistent exceedance of the 10-second target can trigger scheme non-compliance procedures.

SCT Inst Infrastructure — TIPS and RT1

PSPs can access SCT Inst processing through two competing Clearing and Settlement Mechanisms (CSMs):

TIPS — TARGET Instant Payment Settlement

TIPS is operated by the ECB as part of the T2 consolidated platform (Eurosystem). It went live in November 2018.

Settlement model: True gross RTGS — each SCT Inst transaction is settled individually in central bank money in real time. There is no deferred net settlement; each transaction either settles gross or is rejected.

Account structure: PSPs must open a TIPS-DCA (Dedicated Cash Account) on the T2 platform and pre-fund it with central bank money. The TIPS-DCA is the settlement account for instant payments. PSPs can transfer liquidity between their MCA and their TIPS-DCA intraday.

Reachability: TIPS is open to all PSPs connected to T2. PSPs not directly connected can participate as "instructing parties" through a directly connected PSP.
RT1 — EBA CLEARING Real-Time 1

RT1 is operated by EBA CLEARING — the same organisation that operates STEP2. It went live in November 2017, making it the first pan-European instant payment infrastructure.

Settlement model: Near-real-time DNS — transactions are processed in real time (sub-second routing and confirmation) but net positions are settled periodically, approximately every 10 minutes, via T2. This makes RT1 technically a DNS system with very short settlement cycles rather than true RTGS.

Account structure: PSPs require either a RT1 TIPS-DCA (for settlement via T2) or access to the RT1 liquidity pool (a pre-funded pool maintained by RT1 participants for collateral purposes).

Interoperability: A reachability agreement between TIPS and RT1 means that a payment originating from a TIPS-connected PSP can reach a beneficiary at an RT1-connected PSP, and vice versa — providing near-universal reachability across SEPA.

7.6 ISO 20022 Message Suite for SEPA

Message Namespaces — PAIN, PACS, CAMT

📤

PAIN — PAyment INitiation

Used in the customer-to-bank (C2B) space. The customer sends payment instructions to their PSP (pain.001, pain.008) and receives status reports (pain.002). PAIN messages are never used in the interbank space — they stop at the first PSP.

🔄

PACS — PAyment Clearing & Settlement

Used exclusively in the interbank (FI-to-FI) space. Carry actual fund movement instructions between PSPs via STEP2/TIPS/RT1. Core messages: pacs.008 (SCT), pacs.003 (SDD), pacs.004 (Return), pacs.002 (Status), pacs.007 (Reversal), pacs.010 (Settlement Instruction).

💼

CAMT — CAsh ManagemenT

Used in both C2B and interbank spaces. In C2B: account reporting (camt.052, camt.053, camt.054). In interbank: payment cancellation/recall (camt.056) and investigation resolution (camt.029). Customer-side cancellation requests use camt.055.

7.7 STEP2 Interoperability with National ACHs

The PE-ACH Concept

STEP2 was designed as the PE-ACH (Pan-European ACH) — the spine connecting national payment islands across Europe into a single interoperable clearing zone. Prior to SEPA, each country had its own domestic ACH (automated clearing house) and cross-border payments required correspondent banking chains or direct bilateral bank agreements. STEP2's PE-ACH model replaced this fragmented landscape with a single multilateral infrastructure.

However, national ACH systems continue to operate alongside STEP2 — processing domestic transactions more cost-efficiently and in some cases providing additional services (e.g., local instant payment rails, domestic return procedures, national scheme overlays). Interoperability between STEP2 and national ACHs is achieved through bilateral exchange agreements that allow PSPs connected to a national ACH to exchange SEPA payments with STEP2 participants without requiring direct STEP2 membership.

How Interoperability Works Technically

An interoperability gateway sits between the national ACH and STEP2. When a PSP connected to a national ACH initiates a SEPA payment to a beneficiary whose PSP is a direct STEP2 participant, the national ACH routes the pacs.008 instruction through the interoperability gateway to STEP2. STEP2 then routes onward to the beneficiary PSP in the normal way. The interoperability gateway performs format translation if required (ensuring ISO 20022 compliance) and handles settlement reconciliation between the two systems.

PE-ACH Vision: The long-term vision for SEPA is a fully integrated payment area where any PSP in any SEPA country can reach any other PSP through a seamless interoperability layer — regardless of which ACH or CSM each is directly connected to. STEP2 as the pan-European spine, connected bilaterally to national ACHs, and with TIPS and RT1 providing instant payment reachability, realises this vision to a substantial degree. The remaining gaps (PSPs only connected to national ACHs without STEP2 interoperability agreements) are progressively being closed as SEPA payment volumes and the mandatory SCT Inst requirements drive universal reachability.

Recall Reason Codes

CodeNameDescriptionNotes
DUPLDuplicate PaymentThe same payment was submitted and settled twice — either by system error or human error at the originating PSPMost common recall reason. Standard recall (camt.056) or same-day reversal (pacs.007). Beneficiary PSP typically cooperative as beneficiary received funds in error.
FRADFraudulent Credit TransferThe originating PSP has reason to believe the payment was initiated fraudulently — e.g., the customer's credentials were compromised, or the payment was made under APP fraud manipulationRecall window: up to 13 months. Most contested recall type — beneficiary may have already spent funds. Law enforcement referral common. Beneficiary PSP may freeze beneficiary account pending investigation.
TECHTechnical ProblemThe payment was sent due to a technical failure — e.g., a system bug caused wrong amount, wrong beneficiary, or wrong currency to be sentStraightforward from beneficiary PSP's perspective — clear technical error. Usually resolved within the 15-business-day window.
CUSTCustomer RequestThe originating customer has asked their PSP to recall the payment — e.g., goods not delivered, wrong payee, payment made in errorOnly valid for standard recall (camt.056). Beneficiary PSP will check if beneficiary consents. If beneficiary disputes, PSP cannot force return — must advise customer to pursue civil route.

End-to-End Recall Flow — Standard Recall

1

Originating PSP Identifies Issue

PSP A (or its customer via camt.055) identifies that a settled payment must be recalled. PSP A checks: is the recall within 13 months? Is the reason code valid (DUPL, FRAD, TECH, CUST)? PSP A prepares the camt.056 FI to FI Payment Cancellation Request referencing the original pacs.008 Transaction ID and End-to-End ID.

2

camt.056 Sent to Beneficiary PSP (via STEP2 or bilateral)

PSP A sends camt.056 to PSP B. STEP2 routes the message or it is sent via bilateral SWIFT/SIC connectivity depending on the infrastructure arrangement. PSP B receives and acknowledges the recall request.

3

PSP B Contacts Beneficiary Customer (15 Business Days)

PSP B has up to 15 business days to respond. PSP B will: (a) check if the recall reason is valid; (b) contact the beneficiary customer to request consent to return the funds; (c) assess any legal holds or fraud investigations that may apply.

4a

Accepted — PSP B Returns via pacs.004

If the beneficiary agrees (or if FRAD/DUPL is clear-cut), PSP B debits the beneficiary's account and sends a pacs.004 Payment Return to PSP A via STEP2. The pacs.004 references the original transaction. PSP A credits its customer's account upon receipt.

4b

Refused — PSP B Sends camt.029 Negative Response

If PSP B cannot or will not return the funds (beneficiary refuses, funds dissipated, legal dispute), PSP B sends a camt.029 Resolution of Investigation with a negative resolution. PSP A must inform its customer that the recall has been refused. The customer must now pursue civil recovery — legal action against the beneficiary — outside the SEPA scheme framework.

Recall Limitations — What the Scheme Cannot Do: SEPA recall procedures are voluntary cooperation mechanisms. PSP B cannot be forced by the scheme to return funds if the beneficiary refuses. The scheme provides the messaging infrastructure and a defined process, but does not give PSP A any legal right to compel the return of funds. This is the fundamental limitation of push payment recovery. For fraud cases, PSPs increasingly share intelligence via fraud databases and law enforcement channels, but the primary recovery route after a refused recall is civil litigation, which may be impractical for smaller amounts. This is the principal reason why APP fraud remains a major challenge: instant, irrevocable payments combined with voluntary recall mechanisms place the burden of recovery on victims.
